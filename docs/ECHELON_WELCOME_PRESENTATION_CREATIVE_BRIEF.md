## 1. Executive creative direction

### The presentation's job

This is not a sales deck. It is the first guided experience an **approved, enrolled Echelon member** receives after they join. It should make a new member feel that they have entered a private, disciplined performance environment with clear standards, thoughtful support, and a practical first path forward.

The emotional sequence is:

1. **Arrival:** “I made a deliberate decision to join something different.”
2. **Belonging:** “I am part of a collective, but I will be coached as an individual.”
3. **Clarity:** “I know what Echelon expects of me and what I can expect from it.”
4. **Capability:** “I have a clear first-week plan and a system to keep progressing.”
5. **Commitment:** “The standard is mine to uphold.”

### One-line brand proposition

**Echelon Fitness Collective helps driven individuals build strength through structure, training with discipline, accountability, purpose, and a standard that carries into every area of life.**

### Presentation audience

- Approved, paid members who have received a Member Portal invitation.
- Likely a mix of 12-Week Transformation, 1-on-1 Coaching, and Group Fitness participants.
- May be beginners, intermediates, or advanced trainees; never assume a single fitness level.
- The experience must be welcoming without becoming casual, and premium without becoming exclusionary.

### Narrative guardrails

- Lead with **structure**, **standards**, **coaching**, and **progress**, not aesthetics or hype.
- Make every instruction concrete: where to go, what to complete, when to communicate, what happens next.
- Use faith as an authentic internal brand principle, character, discipline, and purpose, not as a demand or a barrier to belonging.
- Do not imply medical, nutrition-therapy, injury-rehabilitation, or guaranteed-outcome claims.
- Avoid calling a member “elite” unless the context is the environment/standard, not a promise about their current body or performance.

## 2. Company overview

### Mission

Echelon exists to help people train with a repeatable structure, build discipline, develop confidence, and gain strength that carries beyond a workout.

### Vision

To become a trusted performance collective where training is not treated as a temporary burst of motivation, but as a durable practice of strength, ownership, and intentional growth.

### Brand story

Echelon positions fitness as more than exercise selection. The site repeatedly frames results as the outcome of a system: thoughtful training, honest effort, repeatable actions, feedback, and a plan worth following. The brand does not sell chaos, extreme promises, or generic challenges. It offers a place to return to the standard, especially when motivation is low.

### Why Echelon exists

- Motivation can start a change; structure makes it last.
- Members benefit from a coach, a visible plan, a personal record, and a clear next action instead of guesswork.
- Strength is treated as physical, mental, personal, and behavioral.
- The community creates energy and accountability, while the portal maintains personal context and privacy.

### Core values and their on-site proof

| Value | What it means | Evidence in current brand copy / experience |
|---|---|---|
| Structure | Reduce guesswork with a plan and repeatable systems. | “Building Strength Through Structure”; prescribed plans; onboarding; weekly review; progress tools. |
| Accountability | Follow-through is coached and visible. | Check-ins, Coach Command, weekly performance review, direct coach thread. |
| Commitment | Progress comes from returning to the work. | “Progress is earned by returning to the standard, especially when motivation is low.” |
| Faith | Character, purpose, and discipline originate internally. | About-page Faith principle: “Strength starts internally. Character, discipline, and purpose drive everything.” |
| Community | Shared energy supports individual progress. | Group Fitness, public free class, media gallery, “The Collective” language. |
| Intentionality | Availability, programming, and communication are curated rather than improvised. | Application review, private payment/invite sequence, protected booking windows, Member Vault. |

### Who Echelon serves

- Driven people who want a plan rather than improvisation.
- Individuals seeking general strength, body recomposition, muscle gain, fat loss, athletic performance, or lifestyle accountability.
- People who value clear coaching, transparent expectations, and a higher personal standard.
- Small private groups (friends, families, teams, organizations) seeking a customized training experience.
- Community members who want a structured group-training entry point.

### What makes Echelon different

1. **Standard before spectacle.** The tone is composed, not performative.
2. **A layered experience.** Public education and group access lead into protected, personal coaching tools.
3. **One system across the journey.** Application, payment, invitation, onboarding, plan, progress, review, and retention are designed as connected steps.
4. **Private coaching infrastructure.** Members have plans, photos, messaging, nutrition tracking, weekly review, and vault content in one hub.
5. **Community without generic programming.** Group training creates energy; individual coaching maintains personal context.

## 3. Brand identity system

### Brand personality

Disciplined, premium, grounded, direct, composed, human, performance-minded, faith-rooted, intentional, and quietly confident.

### Voice and tone

**Voice characteristics**

- Short, declarative, uppercase-led headlines.
- Warm but not overly familiar.
- Direct instructions with purposeful verbs: *build, train, execute, review, return, raise, organize, fuel, evolve, connect.*
- Uses “standard,” “structure,” “collective,” “forward,” “next level,” “intent,” and “progress” as recurring language.

**Tone by moment**

| Moment | Tone | Example direction |
|---|---|---|
| Welcome | Elevated and reassuring | “You are here. Now we build the system.” |
| Instructions | Crisp and clear | “Complete onboarding before your first coached session.” |
| Accountability | Firm, never shaming | “Return to the next right action.” |
| Celebration | Specific and earned | “Progress is the result of the work you repeated.” |
| Health/safety | Calm and responsible | “Share relevant limitations; coaching is not medical care.” |

### Primary palette

Derived from `assets/css/main.css`:

| Role | Hex | Usage |
|---|---:|---|
| Gold accent | `#FFD700` | Key CTA, section tag, numerical marker, divider, active state. Use as an accent, never as a large presentation background fill. |
| Warm near-black | `#0D0D0C` | Primary canvas, hero darkness, dark background. |
| Warm charcoal | `#1A1917` | Cards, panels, layered surfaces. |
| Hairline border | `#2A2822` | Dividers, card outlines, subtle grid. |
| White | `#FFFFFF` | Primary headline and high-priority text. |
| Muted gray | `#AAAAAA` | Supporting copy, metadata, secondary labels. |

### Typography

| Role | Typeface | Character | Recommended presentation use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Display / navigation | Bebas Neue | Tall, condensed, assertive, uppercase. | Slide titles, section labels, numbers, CTAs, tab labels. |
| Body / supporting copy | Montserrat | Clean, modern geometric sans. | Paragraphs, captions, speaker notes, technical information. |

Typography hierarchy:

- Eyebrow / section tag: Montserrat bold, uppercase, generous tracking, gold.
- Hero title: Bebas Neue, uppercase, white, large and compact line-height.
- Supporting statement: Montserrat light/regular, gray-white, restrained line length.
- Number or sequence label: Bebas Neue or Montserrat bold, gold, used sparingly.

### Visual style and design language

- Near-black canvas with warm charcoal layers, not cold blue-black.
- Gold is an indicator of intention: highlights, dividers, selection, sequence, and action.
- Borders are architectural hairlines; cards should feel framed, not pillowy.
- Composition favors asymmetry, high negative space, and full-bleed documentary photography.
- UI references: protected spaces, quiet luxury, precision, journal-like progression, editorial field notes.
- Repeated visual devices: “01 / 02 / 03” sequences, small gold tags, clean horizontal rules, framed photography, dark overlay gradients, single strong CTA.
- Avoid gradients that look neon, excessive rounded rectangles, emoji, generic fitness icons, glossy 3D renders, or bright multicolor charts.

### Motion and animation language

- **Pace:** restrained, deliberate, cinematic, not busy.
- **Entrance:** 250–450ms soft fades, masked image reveals, staggered tag → headline → support sequence.
- **Transitions:** crossfade, slow black-to-image dissolve, horizontal wipe only for “progression” or “system” slides.
- **Micro-motion:** gold rule draws, subtle count-up on 01/02/03, parallax of a single full-bleed image, never multiple moving elements competing.
- **Accessibility:** all motion must have a clean static equivalent; avoid flashing or forced autoplay video.

### Luxury reference calibration

- **Apple:** editorial whitespace, product-like focus, restraint.
- **Nike:** human athletic energy, documentary scale, decisive typography.
- **Porsche:** precision, quiet confidence, materiality, dark contrast.
- **WHOOP:** measurable behavior and recovery context, without overloading data.
- **Equinox:** aspirational physical environment, but Echelon should feel warmer and more community-rooted.
- **Notion:** clear systems, modular organization, frictionless instructions.
- **Tesla:** linear narrative and confidence; avoid a sterile tech aesthetic.

## 5. Membership and coaching experience

### Before joining

1. Visitor explores About, Training Hub, Resources, Shop, Media, and Contact.
2. Visitor may request a free class, join a waitlist, submit a contact inquiry, or apply for coaching.
3. Coaching applicants select 1-on-1, 12-Week Transformation, or Private Group / Organization Wellness.
4. Application asks for current fitness level, goals, availability, commitment, barriers, lifestyle habits, and readiness to follow a structured program.
5. The applicant acknowledges that health screening and liability waiver are required before training begins.

### Approval and enrollment

The protected operational sequence is:

`Application → Admin review → private Stripe payment link → trusted Stripe webhook verifies paid status → Admin sends Member Portal invitation → password setup → onboarding + waiver → Week 1 / first coached experience.`

The Stripe webhook, not a generic success page, is the trusted payment signal. Do not state that a member is active until payment and invitation are actually complete.

### What members see first

The Member Portal opens with **“YOUR STANDARD, ORGANIZED.”** It prioritizes action over a long menu:

1. **Open Coaching:** prescribed work, progress photos, direct coach line.
2. **Log Your Fuel:** food, macros, water, weight, and nutrition targets.
3. **Complete Review:** weekly context for coach adjustments.
4. **Session Check-In:** readiness before a coached session.
5. **Member Vault:** member-only programs, guides, templates, and education.
6. **Onboarding & Waiver:** intentionally lower and collapsible after first-time completion.

### Coaching Workspace

The private Coaching Hub is organized as **Train / Evolve / Connect**:

- Current workout plan.
- Private photo timeline with date and milestone caption.
- Direct coach message thread.
- Clear bridge into Echelon Fuel Tracker.

### Echelon Fuel

The member can:

- Log breakfast, lunch, dinner, and snacks.
- Search food, use recent/saved/common/branded foods, or create a private custom food.
- Track calories, protein, carbs, fat, water, and body weight.
- Set editable daily targets and unit preferences.
- Leave a private daily note about energy, hunger, or routine.
- Review seven-day consistency.

### Weekly Performance

The Weekly Check-In gives the coach context about adherence, recovery, habits, wins, blockers, and next focus. The presentation should frame it as a practical conversation starter, not a report card.

### Support and communication

- Active members can use portal messaging for timely coaching support.
- Email remains appropriate for formal onboarding, lead decisions, and major confirmations.
- The owner/admin uses a response library and Coach Command tasks to maintain follow-through.
- Every template must be personalized; automated language should never feel like an impersonal rejection or promise.

### Scheduling

- Group Fitness follows a published schedule.
- 1-on-1 and Private Group scheduling use protected, released availability, not an always-open calendar.
- Calendly configuration is designed to respect Burn work shifts and the Echelon calendar.
- Use the member-friendly scheduling page when booking links are activated; until then Echelon sends approved links manually.

### Events and community

- Community workouts, drop-ins, special events, and open gym visits use Session Check-In.
- The media gallery shows real moments: group training, running, outdoor sessions, group portraits, and recovery/community energy.
- Private Group Training makes community programmable for small friend/family/team circles.

## 10. Presentation-wide visual production standards

### Layout

- 16:9 widescreen.
- Use a 12-column grid with large outer margins; never let the display type feel cramped.
- Limit each slide to one dominant idea, one dominant image, and one primary action or implication.
- Keep body copy under 35–45 words on most slides; speaker notes carry operational detail.
- Use 01/02/03 numbering for systems, roadmaps, and sequences.

### Background treatment

- Primary: near-black or real Echelon photo under a 60–85% black/near-black gradient overlay.
- Secondary: warm charcoal card surface with gold hairline boundaries.
- Avoid pure white full-screen slides; white may be used in a restrained editorial card only when it supports contrast and brand purpose.

### Iconography

- Use line icons with a technical, minimal weight: plan/clipboard, message, water, check mark, calendar, upload/photo, compass, upward trend.
- White or muted gray by default; gold only for the active/primary state.
- No cartoon fitness icons, flexed-bicep emoji, gradients, or glossy pictograms.

### Accessibility and readability

- Use sufficient contrast: white text on black or heavily darkened photos; gold labels should not carry paragraphs.
- Minimum 22–24pt body text in a 16:9 presentation; avoid tiny “luxury” copy.
- Never place text over visually busy faces or high-detail photo areas.
- Ensure every photo-heavy slide has an accessible text equivalent in speaker notes or alt-text metadata.

### Speaker-note requirements

- Notes should distinguish what is factual/current from optional coach personalization.
- Notes should not include client health details, payment data, passwords, or credentials.
- Notes should remind presenters not to make medical or guaranteed-result claims.
