# ECHELON 12-WEEK BASE MODEL: PERFORMANCE

**Goal:** Athletic conditioning, power, speed, agility, and work capacity for a client training for a sport, event, or general athleticism, not primarily for aesthetics. Strength is trained as a means to power output, not as the end goal.
**Audience:** Intermediate+ clients, ideally with some training base and no unresolved joint/movement red flags, this is the highest-impact, highest-skill-demand program of the six, so it needs more careful screening at intake than the others.
**Format:** 4 days/week, 2 power/speed-emphasis days, 2 strength-emphasis days, plus dedicated warm-up/movement-prep and conditioning blocks built into every session, not bolted on after.
**Status:** Base model, personalize load, plyometric volume, and sport-specific conditioning per client.

---

## 1. Weekly Split

| Day | Focus |
|---|---|
| 1 | Power + Lower Strength |
| 2 | Speed/Agility + Conditioning |
| 3 | Rest or mobility/recovery |
| 4 | Power + Upper Strength |
| 5 | Conditioning + Core/Stability |
| 6 | Optional sport-specific skill work |
| 7 | Rest |

---

## 2. 12-Week Periodization

| Phase | Weeks | Rep Range (strength) | Rest | Intensity | Focus |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1: Movement Prep | 1–3 | 8–10 | 90s | RPE 6–7 | Landing mechanics, jump/sprint technique, base strength |
| 2: Power Build | 4–7 | 4–6 | 120s | RPE 7–8, high bar speed | Explosive strength, higher plyo volume |
| 3: Peak Power | 8–10 | 3–5 | 150s | RPE 8–9, max intent on power work | Highest power output, competition-specificity if applicable |
| Taper | 11 | 4–6 | 120s | RPE 7 | Volume cut, intent stays high, freshness prioritized |
| Deload/Test | 12 | N/A | N/A | N/A | Retest jump, sprint, and strength benchmarks |

**Progression rule:** power work (jumps, throws, sprints) is progressed by intent and quality, not fatigue, every rep is performed at or near maximal effort with full recovery, and a session is cut short the moment bar/jump/sprint quality drops. Strength work underneath it follows a standard load progression once technique is sound.

---

## 3. Day 1: Power + Lower Strength

| Exercise | Sets x Reps | Rest | Targets |
|---|---|---|---|
| Box Jump | 4 x 5 | 90s (full recovery) | Power, Lower Body |
| Trap-Bar Jump (or Trap-Bar Deadlift in Phase 1) | 4 x 3–5 | 150s | Power, Posterior Chain |
| Back Squat | 4 x 4–6 | 120s | Quads, Glutes |
| Bulgarian Split Squat | 3 x 8/leg | 60–75s | Unilateral Strength |
| Nordic Curl (or eccentric-emphasis leg curl) | 3 x 6–8 | 60s | Hamstring Injury Resilience |

**Form cues (Box Jump):** Land soft, knees tracking over toes, absorb through the whole foot, a loud landing is a coaching cue to regress the box height.
**Coaching cues:** "Quality over quantity" on every power rep, the first sign of declining jump height or bar speed ends that exercise for the day.
**Modification down:** Step-up in place of box jump if landing mechanics aren't yet reliable; goblet squat instead of back squat while squat pattern is being built.
**Modification (pregnancy):** Plyometric/jump-based training is not appropriate to assign during pregnancy, redirect to the Weight Loss or a personalized low-impact maintenance plan and flag for the client's own clinician clearance on any continued training.

---

## 4. Day 2: Speed/Agility + Conditioning

| Drill | Volume | Rest | Targets |
|---|---|---|---|
| Acceleration Sprints (10–20m) | 6 x | Full recovery (2–3 min) | Speed |
| Lateral Shuffle / Agility Ladder | 4–6 sets | 60–90s | Agility, Footwork |
| Cone Change-of-Direction Drill | 4–6 x | 90s | Agility |
| Sled Push or Prowler | 4 x 20m | 90s | Power Endurance |
| Conditioning Finisher (short intervals) | 8–10 rounds :20 on/:40 off | N/A | Anaerobic Capacity |

**Coaching cues:** Sprint work is trained fresh, first, and fast, never after a fatiguing strength session, and never for volume at the cost of technique.
**Modification down:** Reduce sprint distance and add more recovery between reps; sub a bike or row for sled work if lower-body loading needs to be managed.
**Modification (pregnancy):** Remove sprinting, agility/cutting drills, and sled work entirely; sub brisk walking intervals or stationary bike at a conversational pace if conditioning work is still desired.

---

## 5. Day 4: Power + Upper Strength

| Exercise | Sets x Reps | Rest | Targets |
|---|---|---|---|
| Medicine Ball Chest Pass | 4 x 5 | 90s | Upper Power |
| Weighted Pull-Up | 4 x 4–6 | 120s | Back, Power-Pulling |
| Barbell Bench Press | 4 x 4–6 | 120s | Chest, Triceps |
| Single-Arm Landmine Press | 3 x 8/side | 60s | Shoulders, Core Stability |
| Face Pull | 3 x 15 | 45s | Shoulder Health |

**Modification down:** Standing medicine ball scoop toss instead of chest pass if throwing power is a new pattern; machine chest press instead of barbell bench while building pressing capacity.

---

## 6. Day 5: Conditioning + Core/Stability

| Exercise | Volume | Rest | Targets |
|---|---|---|---|
| Farmer's Carry | 4 x 30m | 60–90s | Grip, Core, Total-Body Stability |
| Pallof Press | 3 x 12/side | 30s | Anti-Rotation Core |
| Single-Leg RDL | 3 x 8/leg | 45–60s | Balance, Posterior Chain |
| Battle Ropes or Assault Bike Intervals | 8 x :20 on/:40 off | N/A | Conditioning |
| Plank Variations | 3 x 30–45s | 30s | Core Endurance |

**Modification (pregnancy):** Farmer's carry at conservative load and shorter distance if grip/core pressure allows comfortably; standing single-leg balance work instead of single-leg RDL past the point where balance is compromised; battle ropes/assault bike capped at conversational-pace effort, not intervals.

---

## 7. Coach Notes

- **Weekly check-in:** jump height or sprint time trend (retest key benchmarks every 3–4 weeks, not just Week 12), soreness/injury flags, session RPE, sleep quality (recovery is the limiting factor in this program more than any other base model).
- **This program has the lowest error tolerance:** technique on jumping, landing, and sprinting mechanics is coached before any volume or intensity is added, and a client who isn't ready for true plyometric/sprint work should be started on the Muscle or Weight Loss base model instead until foundational strength and movement quality are in place.
- **Personalization starting points:** sport-specific conditioning (energy system demands of a specific sport) is the single biggest lever to individualize here, a soccer player and a powerlifter both fit "Performance" but need very different Day 2/5 conditioning emphasis.
- Any pain, and especially any sharp or joint-related pain during power work, is coached as "stop immediately, and see a clinician", this program's movements carry more injury risk than the others if pushed through pain, so err conservative.
