# ECHELON 12-WEEK BASE MODEL — OLDER-ADULT WELLNESS

**Goal:** Functional strength, joint-friendly mobility, balance, and day-to-day quality of life — "feel better moving through your day," not aesthetics or performance maximization. Falls-prevention and independence are the underlying priorities driving every exercise choice.
**Audience:** Older adult clients (broadly 55+, but really "anyone whose priority is function and joint-friendliness over intensity"), cleared through intake with particular attention to balance history, joint replacements, cardiovascular conditions, and medications that affect exertion or balance.
**Format:** 3 lifting/functional-strength days/week + daily mobility + 2 balance-focused sessions. Lower fatigue cost per session than any other base model — recoverability, not stimulus, is the constraint here.
**Status:** Base model — personalize heavily per client's specific joint history, balance level, and any physician guidance. This is the program where "screen before you program" matters most.

---

## 1. Weekly Split

| Day | Focus |
|---|---|
| 1 | Functional Strength A + Balance |
| 2 | Mobility/Flexibility (10–15 min) |
| 3 | Functional Strength B + Balance |
| 4 | Mobility/Flexibility (10–15 min) |
| 5 | Functional Strength C + Gentle Cardio |
| 6 | Mobility/Flexibility + Walk |
| 7 | Rest |

---

## 2. 12-Week Periodization

| Phase | Weeks | Rep Range | Rest | Intensity (RPE) | Focus |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 — Foundation | 1–4 | 10–15 | 60–90s | 4–5 | Movement confidence, balance baseline, very conservative load |
| 2 — Build | 5–8 | 10–12 | 60s | 5–6 | Gradual load increases, longer balance holds |
| 3 — Sustain | 9–11 | 10–12 | 60s | 6 | Consolidate gains, introduce light functional carries |
| Deload/Reassess | 12 | 12–15 | 90s | 4–5 | Retest balance and functional benchmarks, celebrate progress |

**Progression rule:** progress is measured primarily in quality-of-life terms — can they get up from a chair without hands, climb stairs without holding the rail, carry groceries without strain — alongside modest load increases. Never chase intensity at the expense of a client feeling steady and safe in every rep.

---

## 3. Day 1 — Functional Strength A + Balance

| Exercise | Sets x Reps | Rest | Targets |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sit-to-Stand (chair squat) | 3 x 10–12 | 60s | Quads, Glutes, Functional Strength |
| Seated Row (machine or band) | 3 x 12–15 | 60s | Back, Posture |
| Wall or Incline Push-Up | 3 x 8–12 | 60s | Chest, Triceps |
| Standing Marching (hip flexion) | 3 x 10/leg | 45s | Balance, Hip Strength |
| Single-Leg Stand (hand support as needed) | 3 x 15–20s/side | 30s | Balance |

**Form cues:** Sit-to-stand — lean forward from the hips before rising, push through the whole foot, control the descent rather than dropping into the chair.
**Coaching cues:** "Steady and strong" over "fast and heavy" — every rep in this program is coached for control first.
**Modification up:** Once sit-to-stand is comfortable and controlled, remove hand support or add a light dumbbell held at the chest.
**Modification down:** Keep a hand lightly on a chair or wall for every balance element for as long as needed — there is no rush to remove support, and doing so early is the main safety risk in this program.

---

## 4. Day 3 — Functional Strength B + Balance

| Exercise | Sets x Reps | Rest | Targets |
|---|---|---|---|
| Step-Up (low, stable step) | 3 x 8/leg | 60s | Quads, Glutes, Stair Function |
| Seated or Standing Band Row | 3 x 12–15 | 60s | Back, Posture |
| Seated Overhead Press (light dumbbell or band) | 3 x 10–12 | 60s | Shoulders |
| Heel-to-Toe Walk (hand support as needed) | 3 x 10 steps | 30s | Balance, Gait |
| Standing Hip Abduction (band) | 3 x 12/side | 30s | Hip Stability, Falls Prevention |

**Modification down:** Reduce step height further or use a step with a rail nearby; perform heel-to-toe walk alongside a countertop for continuous light support.

---

## 5. Day 5 — Functional Strength C + Gentle Cardio

| Exercise | Sets x Reps | Rest | Targets |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sit-to-Stand (with slight pause at top) | 3 x 10 | 60s | Quads, Glutes |
| Standing Row (band, both arms) | 3 x 12–15 | 60s | Back |
| Standing Chest Press (band) | 3 x 10–12 | 60s | Chest |
| Farmer's Carry (light, short distance) | 3 x 15–20m | 60s | Grip, Functional Carrying Strength |
| Gentle Cardio (stationary bike or treadmill walk) | 10–15 min, conversational pace | — | Cardiovascular Health |

**Modification down:** Shorten carry distance and use very light load, focused purely on grip and posture rather than a training stimulus; recumbent bike instead of treadmill for anyone with balance concerns during cardio.

---

## 6. Mobility/Flexibility Days (2, 4, 6)

10–15 minutes, gentle and pain-free range of motion for major joints: ankles, hips, shoulders, spine rotation. This is not a stretch-to-the-max session — the goal is maintaining and gradually improving usable range of motion for daily tasks (reaching overhead, turning to check a blind spot, getting in and out of a car), performed well within a comfortable range.

---

## 7. Coach Notes

- **Weekly check-in:** how daily tasks feel (stairs, getting up from the floor or a low chair, carrying groceries), balance confidence self-rating, any new aches or medication changes, sleep.
- **This program answers to a physician's clearance more than any other base model** — confirm any joint replacement precautions, cardiovascular guidance, or balance-affecting medications are known and respected before assigning or progressing anything.
- **Personalization starting points:** balance level varies enormously in this population — some clients need every balance exercise fully supported for the whole 12 weeks, and that is a completely successful outcome, not a failure to progress the template as written.
- **Falls-prevention is the north star.** Any exercise that introduces meaningful fall risk for a specific client (an unsupported single-leg stand for someone with a recent fall history, for example) is modified or removed entirely, not just "made easier."
- Pain, dizziness, or shortness of breath beyond what's expected for gentle exertion is coached as "stop immediately, and see a clinician" — never worked through, and never diagnosed on the spot.
