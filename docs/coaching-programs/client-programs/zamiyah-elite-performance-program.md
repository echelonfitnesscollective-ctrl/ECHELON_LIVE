## Why I Built It This Way

She walked me through all of it on the phone: the lifts she already loves, the hip dips, the arm insecurity, wanting a split by her birthday. Nothing here is generic. The periodization, the arm and core strategy, and the athletic layer are all built around exactly what she told me, so it adds up to the shape she's after by February 26 instead of staying four good days that don't compound.

**On the hip dips:** that "shelf" comes from the upper glute medius/minimus, a different target than the glute max work her hip thrusts and squats already hit. Kept her abduction and clamshell work and added a forward-lean cable angle and a slow pulse variation. Both bias the upper fibers specifically.

**On the "bat wings":** no amount of tricep volume spot-reduces arm fat, that comes down from nutrition and total training volume. What the arm work does is build shape underneath so that as the fat comes off, there's definition waiting, not just skin. That's why Day 3 stays higher-rep and lighter-load: shape, not size.

**On "not big, just bigger glutes":** heavy compound lower-body work paired with a controlled diet builds the glutes preferentially while the rest of her leans out. That's recomposition, and it's exactly what she said on the call she wants.

**On the athletic piece:** plyometric and agility work is loaded in small, high-quality doses on Days 1, 2, and 4. Enough to build real jump and change-of-direction capacity without adding junk volume on top of her lifting.

## Day 1 / Monday — Glute Power + Hip-Dip Shelf
*Heavy · ~55–65 min*

**Warm-Up**
- Glute Bridges — 2 × 15
- Banded Lateral Walks — 2 × 15 steps/side
- Clamshells (bodyweight) — 2 × 15/side
- Pogo Hops (new, primes landing mechanics) — 2 × 20

**Main Lifts**

| Exercise | Sets × Reps | Load |
|---|---|---|
| Hip Thrust | 4 × 6–8 | 45 lb |
| Cable Kickback | 3 × 8–10/leg | 25 lb |
| Romanian Deadlift | 3 × 8–10 | 25 lb |
| Step-Ups (lean-forward, glute bias) | 3 × 10/leg | 15 lb |

Cues: Hip thrust, ribs down, chin tucked, drive through heels, not a lower-back movement. RDL, hips travel back not down, soft knee bend, feel the stretch in the hamstring before reversing. Step-up, torso leans forward over the front leg so the glute, not the quad, does the driving.

**Hip-Dip Shelf Finisher (superset)**

| Exercise | Sets × Reps | Load |
|---|---|---|
| 5A · Standing Cable Abduction, torso leaned forward | 3 × 15–20/leg | 40 lb |
| 5B · Cable Clamshells | 3 × 12–15/side | 10 lb |
| 5C · Side-Lying Hip Abduction Pulses (new) | 2 × 20/side | bodyweight |

The lean-forward angle and the slow pulse both bias the upper glute medius fibers, the muscle that fills in the hip dip. Non-negotiable even on short-time days.

**Athletic Finisher (2×/week)**
- Lateral Bounds — 3 × 6/side
- Broad Jump to Stick — 3 × 5

Three angles (pushdown, overhead, skull crusher) cover the whole triceps without heavy loading. Builds shape, not size; the fat loss that reveals it happens through nutrition, not more sets.

## Day 5 / At Home — Mobility, Splits & Light Recovery
*Light · full flow ~25–30 min*

Her flow, held in sequence, two rounds through. Hold times are the floor; add 5–10 seconds per position every two weeks as range opens up.

1. **Cossack Squats** — 10 each side, dynamic, keep it moving
2. **Butterfly Stretch** — 1 min: contract 5 sec, relax, sink deeper, repeat 3×
3. **Side Lunge Hold** — 45 sec each side
4. **Frog Stretch** — 1–2 min
5. **Pancake Stretch** — 1 min: track fingertip-to-floor distance monthly
6. **Middle Split Lean** — 30–45 sec
7. **Wall-Assisted Middle Split** — 1 min: lower the support height as range improves

**Optional Light Cardio:** 15–20 min incline walk, or light jump rope, low-impact enough to arrive fresh for Monday.

## Nutrition Protocol

Recomposition happens on the plate more than on the platform. At 154 lb, here's the target, built around what she told me on the call she wants: more protein, tighter carbs, more water.

| Macro | Target |
|---|---|
| Protein | 150 g/day |
| Fat | 55 g/day |
| Carbs | 175–225 g/day |
| Water | 90–100 oz/day |

- **Phases 1 & 2 (Weeks 1–15):** hold near 2,000 kcal with the macros above; carbs toward the higher end (~225g), concentrated around Days 1 and 4 when lifting heaviest.
- **Phase 3 (Weeks 16–23):** step down to roughly 1,750–1,800 kcal by trimming carbs toward ~175g. Protein and fat stay put; this is where the deficit does its work without costing strength.
- **Phase 4 (Weeks 24–28):** back up to ~1,900 kcal so she's recovered and full, not depleted, going into her birthday.
- Protein at every meal: chicken, fish, eggs, Greek yogurt, cottage cheese, lean beef. The one number that matters most for keeping the muscle being built while the fat comes off.
- Water climbs on Day 1 and Day 4; add 16–24 oz on top of baseline on heavy lift days.
- Sleep is currently averaging 6 hours, that's the quiet limiter on both glute recovery and appetite control. Even pushing toward 7 hours will show up faster than any dietary change.

*General fitness and nutrition guidance from her coach, not a substitute for individualized clinical dietary advice. Check with a physician before starting the plyometric or splits work if there is any joint, hip, or back history.*

## Mobility & Splits: Frequency, Not Just a Day

One session a week won't produce a split by February; range of motion responds to frequency. Keep Day 5 as the full flow, and add two short 10-minute "flexibility snacks" on off-evenings (pancake fold + middle split lean is enough) to triple weekly stretching frequency without adding a real session.

- Check fingertip-to-floor distance on the pancake stretch monthly, that's the clearest progress marker.
- Photograph the wall-assisted middle split monthly from the same angle; lower the support height as depth improves.
- Never stretch into sharp or pinching pain; a deep stretch sensation is the target, pain is the stop signal.
