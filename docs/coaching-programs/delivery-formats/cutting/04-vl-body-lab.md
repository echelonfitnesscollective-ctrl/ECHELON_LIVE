# CUTTING IN VL BODY LAB

**Base program:** [Cutting — 12-Week Base Model](../../01-cutting-12-week.md)
**Format:** High-energy athletic group class · see [VL Body Lab Blueprint](../../blueprint-04-vl-body-lab.md) for the general session shape

---

## How the base model translates here

Unlike a general Weight Loss client, a Cutting client is often already a natural fit for VL Body Lab rather than a format they need to "earn" their way into. The Cutting base model assumes an intermediate+ client with an existing training base, RPE 6-9 across its phases, and full-body metabolic conditioning built directly into the weekly split — that's a much closer match to VL Body Lab's power, strength, and conditioning structure than the Weight Loss base model's deliberately lower-intensity, habit-first approach. A trained client cutting down for a physique goal frequently has both the work capacity and the movement competency VL Body Lab's power/speed block assumes.

**What carries over, what doesn't:**
- The base model's strength and full-body conditioning emphasis fits VL Body Lab's structure well, and the intensity ranges are much closer aligned than for Weight Loss.
- The base model's density-method progression rule — the specific mechanism that protects muscle at a steep deficit by tightening rest as reps hit target — does **not** carry over as precisely. VL Body Lab's circuit-style, group-appropriate loading can't track individual working loads with the precision the base model's progression rule assumes.
- The base model's recovery-sensitive coaching (deload triggers from rising resting heart rate + falling sleep quality) still needs to happen somewhere — VL Body Lab's format doesn't include the individualized weekly check-in that catches those signals, so this needs to be tracked elsewhere in the client's week (1-on-1 check-ins, or self-reported through Today's Work).

## Personalization decision table

| Intake field | What it changes in VL Body Lab |
|---|---|
| **Delivery setting** | The room's live, high-intensity format replaces the base model's precise load tracking with real-time coach cueing — appropriate for most Cutting clients given their training base, but the deficit-driven recovery risk still needs a check-in mechanism outside the room. |
| **Primary goal** | A stated Cutting goal (versus general Weight Loss) is itself a strong positive signal for readiness here — it implies an existing training base and tolerance for higher intensity that the Weight Loss population, on average, doesn't yet have. |
| **Secondary goal** | A performance or athletic secondary goal reinforces VL Body Lab as a strong fit — the power/speed and conditioning blocks directly support that secondary emphasis alongside the primary cutting goal. |
| **Age** | Older clients cutting through VL Body Lab still default to the class's conservative regressions on the power/speed block, same as any format — training experience doesn't override landing-mechanics and joint-load caution for this population. |
| **Training experience** | Still the primary gate, but the bar is lower to clear here than for a general Weight Loss client, since the Cutting base model already assumes intermediate+ status. A client without genuine familiarity with jumps, sprints, or loaded circuit work should still start on the built-in regressions for their first several sessions regardless of strength level elsewhere. |
| **Training days available** | If VL Body Lab is the client's primary or sole training format, confirm weekly volume and recovery are sufficient to support both the deficit and the format's intensity — this combination (steep deficit + high-intensity group training) has less recovery buffer than either alone, so watch total weekly load carefully. |
| **Session duration** | Fixed at 50 minutes by the room — not adjustable per member. |
| **Equipment access** | Not applicable — the room's equipment is fixed. |
| **Current activity level** | Most Cutting clients arrive active enough for this format; a recent activity dip (injury layoff, life disruption) is still a reason to default to the built-in regressions for the first few sessions before resuming full intensity. |
| **Injuries, conditions** | Per the VL Body Lab blueprint, plyometric and sprint work is not appropriate during pregnancy, and any joint or landing pain is coached as "stop immediately, see a clinician" — a Cutting goal never overrides this, and a steep deficit's reduced recovery capacity is an additional reason for caution around any flagged joint issue. |
| **Medical clearance** | Anything short of full clearance is a hard stop for the power/speed block specifically, same as any other goal — confirm with the coach before class whether strength and conditioning blocks are still appropriate with modification. |
| **Exercise preferences** | Minor lever — worth knowing so a disliked movement is swapped for its built-in regression rather than avoided or pushed through. |
| **Sleep and stress** | This matters more for a Cutting client here than for almost any other combination in the system — an aggressive deficit already reduces recovery capacity, and VL Body Lab is the highest-intensity group format available. Poor sleep or high stress is a direct, non-negotiable cue to scale the power block down that day. |
| **Biggest consistency barrier** | If the barrier is "boredom" or "needing intensity to stay engaged," VL Body Lab is frequently the right long-term home for a Cutting client specifically — the intensity that makes it a poor starting point for many Weight Loss clients is often exactly what keeps a Cutting client consistent. |

## Coach notes

- Don't default to treating VL Body Lab as an earned or graduated format for a Cutting client the way it's positioned for Weight Loss — screen training experience honestly, but expect most Cutting-track members to clear that bar on arrival rather than needing to work up to it.
- The real watch-item for this combination isn't readiness, it's recovery: a steep deficit plus a high-intensity format compounds fatigue faster than either alone. Track sleep, resting heart rate, and strength trend closely, and don't hesitate to recommend a temporary format swap to Group Fitness or Faith & Favor Mobility during a scheduled refeed or diet break.
