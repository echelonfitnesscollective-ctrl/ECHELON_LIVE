# CUTTING IN GROUP FITNESS

**Base program:** [Cutting: 12-Week Base Model](../../01-cutting-12-week.md)
**Format:** Recurring class · see [Group Fitness Blueprint](../../blueprint-01-group-fitness.md) for the general session shape

---

## How the base model translates here

Be honest about what Group Fitness can and can't deliver for a Cutting client before positioning it. The Cutting base model is built on precise, progressive load management, 4 lifting days a week with defined rep ranges, a density-based progression rule, and rest periods tightened as strength improves, all of it aimed at protecting hard-earned muscle at a meaningfully steeper deficit than general weight loss. A fixed, published class rotation, run for a room of 12-15 people at once, cannot track and progress an individual's working loads with that precision. Group Fitness is not the format that replaces the base model's structured lifting days for a Cutting client.

**What carries over, what doesn't:**
- The base model's full-body strength and metabolic conditioning *movement patterns* map reasonably well onto Group Fitness's rotation.
- The base model's actual engine, individualized load progression and the density-method progression rule that drives muscle retention at a steep deficit, does **not** carry over. A class can't track one member's working weights closely enough to run that rule.
- Group Fitness works best here as a **conditioning and consistency supplement** alongside a primary Cutting delivery format (1-on-1 or 12-Week Transformation), not as the client's main training stimulus.

**Mapping the base model's split onto the rotation:**

| Cutting base model day | Group Fitness rotation slot |
|---|---|
| Upper/Lower Body Strength | Week 1 (Full-Body Strength Circuit) and Week 3 (Strength + Core Focus), useful as *additional* volume, not a substitute for the client's primary strength days |
| Full-Body Metabolic Conditioning | Week 2 (Metabolic Conditioning) |
| Conditioning Finisher | Week 4 (Mixed Format) can double as an extra conditioning day if the client's weekly volume has room for it |
| Posterior Chain + Core | Not directly covered, flag to the client that this needs to happen elsewhere in their week |

## Personalization decision table

| Intake field | What it changes in Group Fitness |
|---|---|
| **Delivery setting** | Group Fitness's fixed room and published schedule mean live scaling and coach judgment replace a written, individualized plan, the intake profile is what turns generic room programming into an informed, safety-aware session for a Cutting client specifically. |
| **Primary goal** | A stated Cutting goal is the cue to check, before class, whether this member has a primary strength stimulus happening elsewhere in their week, if Group Fitness is their *only* training, that's a conversation, not something to quietly work around. |
| **Secondary goal** | A performance or strength secondary goal reinforces pointing this member toward the scale-up options on every exercise rather than the standard version, they're likely under-stimulated by the room's default intensity. |
| **Age** | Same principle as any format: older members get conservative load and impact recommendations by default, but age alone doesn't override an otherwise well-trained Cutting client's readiness for real intensity. |
| **Training experience** | The most load-bearing field here. An intermediate+ Cutting client (which the base model assumes) should default to the scale-up on strength blocks without being asked, the room's standard load is likely under-dosed for someone actively trying to preserve trained muscle at a deficit. |
| **Training days available** | If Group Fitness is genuinely the client's only structured training, this is the clearest signal that Group Fitness alone can't support a real cutting phase, recommend 1-on-1 or 12-Week Transformation as the primary format, with Group Fitness as a conditioning add-on. |
| **Session duration** | Fixed at 45-50 minutes by the room, not adjustable per member; note if this consistently leaves the client's weekly strength volume short. |
| **Equipment access** | Not applicable, the room's equipment is fixed. |
| **Current activity level** | Less central here than for a beginner weight-loss client, most Cutting clients arrive with an established activity base. Still worth confirming they're not returning from a layoff before defaulting to scale-up. |
| **Injuries, conditions** | Flag to the coach teaching that day before class, same standard as every other format, a Cutting client pushing hard loads with an unflagged injury is a real risk, not a hypothetical one. |
| **Medical clearance** | Anything short of full clearance means conservative scaling by default, regardless of training experience or how aggressive the stated cutting goal is. |
| **Exercise preferences** | Minor lever (room programming is fixed) but worth knowing, swap proactively rather than letting a disliked movement erode a member's willingness to push intensity that day. |
| **Sleep and stress** | Especially relevant for a Cutting client already managing a steep deficit, poor sleep or high stress on top of aggressive dieting is a cue to suggest that day's standard (not scaled-up) version, since recovery capacity is already reduced. |
| **Biggest consistency barrier** | If it's "time," weigh whether Group Fitness's fixed schedule is the client's only realistic training access, if so, it needs to be supplemented with structured strength work elsewhere, or the cutting goal itself needs to be scaled back to match what's realistically achievable in the available time. |

## Coach notes

- Position Group Fitness honestly with a Cutting client: it's a strong conditioning and consistency supplement, and a legitimate way to add volume, but it is not a substitute for the base model's individually-progressed lifting days. A Cutting client relying on Group Fitness alone is very likely to lose more muscle than the base model intends.
- If a member's stated goal is Cutting and Group Fitness is their only training format, that's the moment to have the 1-on-1 or 12-Week Transformation conversation directly, not to quietly try to make the room work harder than it can for one person.
