# BULKING IN GROUP FITNESS

**Base program:** [Bulking: 12-Week Base Model](../../03-bulking-12-week.md)
**Format:** Recurring class · see [Group Fitness Blueprint](../../blueprint-01-group-fitness.md) for the general session shape

---

## How the base model translates here

Group Fitness is a real mismatch for what a bulking phase actually needs, and it's worth being honest about that upfront rather than stretching the format to fit. The Bulking base model is built around a 4-day upper/lower split with linear load progression on primary compounds, higher weekly volume than a cutting phase, and minimal conditioning so recovery capacity stays reserved for lifting. Group Fitness runs the opposite way: a shared room on a fixed 4-week rotation, moderate circuit-style loading, and a finisher block that adds conditioning volume, exactly the kind of extra fatigue the base model deliberately avoids.

What Group Fitness *can* still deliver for a bulking client is a real, heavy-compound stimulus on the weeks the rotation lines up with strength work, it just can't deliver the individualized, progressively loaded volume a genuine bulk depends on to keep growing week over week.

**Mapping the base model's split onto the rotation:**

| Bulking base model day | Group Fitness rotation slot |
|---|---|
| Upper/Lower compound-focused days | Week 1 (Full-Body Strength Circuit) and Week 3 (Strength + Core Focus), push these members toward the heaviest load the room's equipment and format allow |
| Weak-point accessory day | Not a class format, coach separately, or point toward 1-on-1/Transformation for a real accessory day |
| N/A | Week 2 (Metabolic Conditioning) and Week 4 (Mixed Format) add conditioning volume that works against a bulking client's recovery budget, flag this rather than treating it as automatically beneficial |

A bulking client attending Group Fitness gets *a* heavy training stimulus on the strength-themed weeks, delivered live instead of through a tracked, progressively loaded plan, a genuine bonus day or on-ramp, not a substitute for the base model's actual training split.

## Personalization decision table

Group Fitness can't rewrite the room's programming per person, but intake data still drives real, individual decisions, mostly around scaling, load selection, and whether this format should be a bulking client's primary training at all.

| Intake field | What it changes in Group Fitness |
|---|---|
| **Secondary goal** | A bulking client whose secondary goal is Performance or general conditioning gets more benefit from this format's conditioning weeks than a pure-bulking client, worth naming that distinction so the client understands why the coach isn't discouraging every conditioning week for them specifically. |
| **Training experience** | New lifters get pointed to the room's standard load and floor-level scale by default; experienced lifters chasing real hypertrophy should be steered toward the heaviest weight the room's equipment supports on strength weeks, and told plainly that this format alone won't progress them the way the base model's tracked loading does. |
| **Training days available** | If Group Fitness is a bulking client's *only* structured training, that's the clearest signal in this whole table, the base model's 4-day split and progressive loading genuinely need a format built to deliver them; recommend 1-on-1 or 12-Week Transformation rather than relying on Group Fitness alone. |
| **Equipment access** | Not applicable, the room's equipment is fixed. |
| **Current activity level** | Less relevant here than for other goals, since the base program already requires a training base, a bulking client showing up to Group Fitness is rarely training from zero, but confirm activity level hasn't dropped since intake before assuming full readiness for the room's heaviest option. |
| **Injuries, conditions, pregnancy/postpartum** | Flag to the coach teaching that day before class, not during, and remember the base model itself flags that Bulking is not an appropriate goal during pregnancy; a pregnant member with a Bulking profile needs a goal conversation, not just a modified class. |
| **Medical clearance** | "Pending" or "not yet discussed" means conservative loading by default on the strength weeks until resolved. |
| **Exercise preferences** | Minor lever given fixed room programming, but worth knowing, e.g., a client who dislikes a specific press variation can be shown the room's swap proactively. |
| **Sleep and stress** | A rough sleep/high-stress week is a specific cue to skip that day's conditioning-heavy rotation slot if one is scheduled, and stick to the strength-themed weeks only, protecting recovery matters more for a bulking client than for most other goals in this room. |
| **Biggest consistency barrier** | If it's "time" and Group Fitness's schedule is genuinely the only training window that fits, be direct that this format alone will produce slower, less predictable size gains than the base model's tracked split, a real trade worth naming, not hiding. |

## Coach notes

- Be straightforward with bulking clients about what this format can and can't do for their specific goal, it's a legitimate bonus training day or entry point back into consistent training, not a substitute for the individualized progressive-loading program the goal actually requires.
- A bulking client who's only doing Group Fitness and wants real size gains is the clearest "this client needs a different format" conversation in the whole Bulking lineup, steer that conversation toward 1-on-1 Coaching or 12-Week Transformation early rather than letting months pass on a format that was never built to deliver this goal.
