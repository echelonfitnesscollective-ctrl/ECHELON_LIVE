# BULKING IN VL BODY LAB

**Base program:** [Bulking — 12-Week Base Model](../../03-bulking-12-week.md)
**Format:** High-energy athletic group class · see [VL Body Lab Blueprint](../../blueprint-04-vl-body-lab.md) for the general session shape

---

## How the base model translates here

This pairing has a real tension that's worth naming directly rather than smoothing over: VL Body Lab is built around power, speed, and athletic conditioning across three demanding blocks in a single 50-minute session, while the Bulking base model deliberately keeps conditioning to a minimum specifically so recovery capacity stays reserved for lifting and growth. Those two designs are pulling in different directions on the same finite resource — a bulking client's weekly recovery budget.

This is a different situation than it looks like for Weight Loss, where VL Body Lab was framed as "a higher-intensity delivery of the same goal, not a mismatch." For Bulking, the mismatch is more structural: the base model's whole training philosophy is built on *not* spending recovery on conditioning, and VL Body Lab's Power/Speed and Conditioning Finisher blocks do exactly that. A bulking client training here regularly is, in effect, running a second recovery-demanding program on top of the base model's already higher-than-cutting volume — a real risk of under-recovering, stalling load progression, and blunting the very growth the surplus was meant to fuel.

**What carries over, what doesn't:**
- The Strength Block fits reasonably well — group-appropriate compound loading in the same spirit as the base model's primary lifts.
- The Power/Speed Block and Conditioning Finisher do **not** carry over as a good fit for an active bulking client — they add fatigue and calorie expenditure the base model's design is specifically trying to avoid.
- This means VL Body Lab works best for a bulking client as an occasional, capped-frequency addition (an athletic outlet or a change of pace), not a regular weekly training slot the way it can be for other goals.

## Personalization decision table

| Intake field | What it changes in VL Body Lab |
|---|---|
| **Secondary goal** | The clearest gate in this table — a Bulking client with a genuine Performance or athletic secondary goal has a real reason to be here regularly; a client bulking with no secondary athletic goal is mostly adding recovery cost for limited return toward their stated primary goal. |
| **Training experience** | New/beginner lifters with a Bulking goal should default to every regression on the Power/Speed block, same standard as any other goal — but for Bulking specifically, also weigh whether they're ready for this block's fatigue cost on top of a lifting-focused base program at all. |
| **Current activity level** | Independent of experience level — a bulking client already training hard 4+ days on the base model has less spare recovery capacity for this format than the same client would on a lower-volume goal; factor total weekly training load, not just fitness level, into how often this format is appropriate. |
| **Age** | Same conservative-regression defaults as other goals for landing mechanics and joint load — for a bulking client this compounds with the base model's already-higher weekly tonnage, so err toward less frequent VL Body Lab attendance for older lifters carrying real lifting volume elsewhere. |
| **Injuries, conditions, pregnancy/postpartum** | Per the VL Body Lab blueprint, plyometric and sprint work is not appropriate during pregnancy — and the base model itself already redirects Bulking away from pregnancy entirely, so this scenario shouldn't reach this format in the first place. Any joint or landing pain is coached as "stop immediately, see a clinician," same as every format. |
| **Medical clearance** | Anything short of full clearance is a hard stop on the power/speed block; the strength block may still be appropriate with modification, confirmed with the coach before class. |
| **Equipment access** | Not applicable — the room's equipment is fixed. |
| **Sleep and stress** | The most important field in this table for a bulking client — poor sleep or high stress is already a cue in the base model to hold load rather than progress; layering VL Body Lab's fatigue on top of a rough recovery week is the clearest scenario to skip this format entirely that week, not just scale it down. |
| **Biggest consistency barrier** | If the barrier is "boredom" with a pure lifting split, a capped, occasional VL Body Lab session can genuinely help long-term adherence — worth naming as a deliberate, limited addition rather than a regular training day. |

## Coach notes

- Treat VL Body Lab as an occasional addition for a Bulking client, not a default recurring slot — the format's fatigue cost works against the base model's core design more directly here than for almost any other goal in the lineup.
- If a bulking client wants VL Body Lab as a regular part of their week, have the direct conversation about trade-offs: either the lifting split's volume or progression pace comes down to make room, or the goal itself may be better served by leaning toward the Performance track instead of Bulking.
- A client whose primary interest is clearly athletic performance and size is secondary is often better served reconsidering their primary goal entirely, rather than trying to run a full bulking program alongside frequent VL Body Lab attendance.
