# OLDER-ADULT WELLNESS IN GROUP FITNESS

**Base program:** [Older-Adult Wellness — 12-Week Base Model](../../06-older-adult-wellness-12-week.md)
**Format:** Recurring class · see [Group Fitness Blueprint](../../blueprint-01-group-fitness.md) for the general session shape

---

## How the base model translates here

Group Fitness's published 4-week rotation was not designed around this population's needs first — it was designed for a general membership with a scaling philosophy layered on top. That scaling philosophy is genuinely necessary here, not optional: an Older-Adult Wellness client in Group Fitness should never simply be doing "the same class, but slower." The room's coach carries real responsibility for making sure the floor-level scale is actually offered, actually visible, and actually appropriate — not assumed to exist because the class technically has a scale-down option.

**Mapping the base model's split onto the rotation:**

| Older-Adult Wellness base model day | Group Fitness rotation slot | Fit |
|---|---|---|
| Functional Strength A/B/C + Balance | Week 1 (Full-Body Strength Circuit) and Week 3 (Strength + Core Focus) | Workable, with the floor-level scale as the default starting point, not an opt-in |
| Mobility/Flexibility days | Not a class format — coached separately, at home or via Today's Work | — |
| Gentle Cardio | Week 2 (Metabolic Conditioning) | Requires real caution — see below |
| — | Week 4 (Mixed Format / Community Challenge) | Needs individual judgment per client; partner work and competitive framing don't fit every client here |

**Where this format genuinely struggles for this population:** Week 2's Metabolic Conditioning theme and Week 4's competitive Mixed Format are both built around elevated heart rate and pace — the opposite of this base model's lowest-fatigue-cost, control-first design. These weeks are not automatically off-limits, but they require the coach to actively confirm the floor-level version is genuinely low-impact and self-paced, not just "the same circuit with lighter weights."

## Personalization decision table

| Intake field | What it changes in Group Fitness |
|---|---|
| **Delivery setting** | Group Fitness's fixed room and published rotation mean personalization happens entirely through live scaling and pre-class coach briefing — there is no written individual plan to fall back on here. |
| **Primary goal** | Confirms this client should default to the floor-level scale on every exercise in the room, every week, regardless of how the rotation theme reads that day. |
| **Secondary goal** | Rarely changes much in Group Fitness specifically — the format doesn't have the room to blend goals the way 1-on-1 does — but worth noting for the coach's awareness. |
| **Age** | The room's default assumption should invert for anyone flagged in this category: start conservative and let the client ask for more, rather than starting standard and scaling down only if requested. |
| **Training experience** | New/beginner clients in this population should be introduced to the room's support options (chairs, walls, rails) proactively, before class starts, not left to discover they're allowed to use them. |
| **Training days available** | If Group Fitness is the client's only structured activity, the base model's daily mobility work and balance-focused days aren't being covered by the room alone — flag this as a gap to fill with Today's Work between classes. |
| **Session duration** | Not adjustable within a fixed class length — if 45-50 minutes is too long for a given client's current capacity, that's a signal to consider 1-on-1 or Faith & Favor Mobility instead, not to push through a full class. |
| **Equipment access** | Not applicable — the room's equipment is fixed. |
| **Current activity level** | Sedentary or deconditioned clients should default to the most conservative scale for at least their first several sessions, independent of what the room's theme is doing that day — this population's ramp-up needs to be slower than the standard Group Fitness on-ramp. |
| **Injuries, conditions** | The single most important field for this format, same as any other goal category — flag joint replacements, arthritis, or balance-affecting conditions to the teaching coach *before* class, not mid-circuit. |
| **Medical clearance** | Anything short of full, confirmed clearance means conservative-only participation until resolved — this population's clearance bar is the highest in the system, and Group Fitness's live, unsupervised-between-corrections format makes this especially important to confirm before, not during, class. |
| **Exercise preferences** | Minor lever given fixed room programming, but useful — knowing a client dislikes or fears a specific movement (often floor-to-standing transitions) lets the coach offer the alternative proactively. |
| **Sleep and stress** | A rough sleep week or new medication is a cue to check in before class and default to the lighter scale that day, especially given this population's higher sensitivity to fatigue and balance-affecting factors. |
| **Biggest consistency barrier** | If it's "intimidation" or fear of not keeping up, that's a strong signal Group Fitness may not be the right entry point at all — Faith & Favor Mobility or 1-on-1 are usually a gentler, more honest starting place. |

## Coach notes

- **Do not assume the floor-level scale is self-evidently available.** For this population specifically, state the modification out loud, every exercise, every class — the base model's own coach notes are explicit that removing support early is the main safety risk in this program, and a group setting makes it easy for a client to quietly skip the modification to keep up with the room.
- If an Older-Adult Wellness client's intake shows a recent fall, unconfirmed clearance, or multiple flagged conditions, Group Fitness alone is likely not the appropriate primary format — that's a conversation for 1-on-1 Coaching or Faith & Favor Mobility, not a reason to place extra caution on the group coach and hope it's enough.
- Balance and mobility work genuinely suffer in a group setting compared to 1-on-1 or Faith & Favor — treat Group Fitness as a reasonable strength-and-community complement for this population, not their primary source of balance training.
