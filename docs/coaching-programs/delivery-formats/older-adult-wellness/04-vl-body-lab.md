# OLDER-ADULT WELLNESS IN VL BODY LAB

**Base program:** [Older-Adult Wellness — 12-Week Base Model](../../06-older-adult-wellness-12-week.md)
**Format:** High-energy athletic group class · see [VL Body Lab Blueprint](../../blueprint-04-vl-body-lab.md) for the general session shape

---

## How the base model translates here — honestly, mostly it doesn't

Be direct with clients about this one: VL Body Lab is not an appropriate primary or default delivery format for most Older-Adult Wellness clients, and this isn't a "start scaled down and work your way up" situation the way it can be for a deconditioned younger adult on the Weight Loss track. The two programs are built on opposite premises. VL Body Lab's Power/Speed block is explicitly built around jumps, sprints, and explosive med-ball work — its blueprint calls it "the highest-injury-risk block if scaled wrong" even for its intended athletic-conditioning population. The Older-Adult Wellness base model is explicitly built around the opposite premise: falls-prevention as the north star, lowest fatigue cost per session of any base model, and any exercise introducing meaningful fall risk is removed entirely, not just scaled down.

Those two design philosophies don't reconcile with a regression. A step-up instead of a box jump is a real, legitimate regression for a healthy adult building toward more power work. For most Older-Adult Wellness clients, the entire format — the pace, the unpredictability of a group class environment during explosive movement, the landing mechanics even a "regressed" plyometric variation still demands — sits outside what this base model is designed to ask of a client's joints and balance system.

**The narrow exception, stated honestly:** a genuinely athletic older adult — someone with a real, current training background, full and unrestricted physician clearance, no balance or fall history, and no relevant joint or cardiovascular precautions — can be a legitimate fit for VL Body Lab. That client's program may not need to route through the Older-Adult Wellness base model at all; age alone doesn't put someone in this program if their actual training capacity looks more like the Performance base model's intended population. This is a case-by-case judgment made with the coach directly, not a default path, and not something to assume based on how a client "seems" in a brief conversation.

## Personalization decision table

| Intake field | What it changes in VL Body Lab |
|---|---|
| **Delivery setting** | The group, live-pace format is itself the primary mismatch for this population — even a well-scaled individual exercise happens inside a room moving at a pace this base model is not designed to ask a client to match. |
| **Primary goal** | An Older-Adult Wellness primary goal (function, falls-prevention, joint-friendliness) is a strong signal against this format by default, not a scaling question — the goal and the format's design premise are in tension from the start. |
| **Secondary goal** | The one place a secondary goal matters here: if a client's *actual* intake profile reads closer to Performance (a real training background, high current activity, no relevant precautions) with age simply being a demographic fact rather than a functional limitation, that secondary signal is worth a direct conversation about whether Older-Adult Wellness is even the right primary base model for them. |
| **Age** | Age alone is never the deciding factor either direction — a 58-year-old with a joint replacement and no training history is a clearer "not this format" case than an active, cleared 72-year-old with a real strength-training background, and the decision should be made on the full profile, not the birth year. |
| **Training experience** | Anything short of a genuine, current strength and conditioning background is a firm redirect away from this format — there is no "beginner track" within VL Body Lab appropriate for this population's typical starting point. |
| **Current activity level** | Sedentary, lightly active, or even moderately active-but-untested clients should not start here — steer toward Group Fitness, 1-on-1, or Faith & Favor Mobility, with VL Body Lab revisited only if activity level, confidence, and clearance all genuinely support it later. |
| **Injuries, conditions** | Per the VL Body Lab blueprint, plyometric and high-impact work is treated as a hard stop for anyone with a relevant joint, balance, or landing-mechanics concern — for this population, that describes the large majority of clients, not an edge case. |
| **Medical clearance** | Full, specific, unrestricted clearance for high-impact and explosive work is required, not general clearance to exercise — this is a materially higher bar than the base model's own clearance requirement, and it should be treated as such. |
| **Equipment access** | Not applicable — the room's equipment is fixed. |
| **Exercise preferences** | Rarely the deciding factor here; the format-level mismatch above almost always outweighs individual movement preferences for this population. |
| **Sleep and stress** | Even for the narrow-exception client who is otherwise appropriate for this format, a poor sleep or high-stress week is a same-day reason to scale down the power block specifically or sit out that block entirely — this format has the least margin for "push through it" in the whole system. |
| **Biggest consistency barrier** | If a client names "wanting intensity" or "boredom with gentler formats" as their barrier, that's worth exploring honestly, but the answer is more often a conversation about VL Body Lab's actual demands (and, if genuinely appropriate, a possible transition toward the Performance track) than an assumption that this format can be safely scaled down to meet them. |

## Coach notes

- Default to redirecting Older-Adult Wellness clients toward Group Fitness, 1-on-1, or Faith & Favor Mobility. This is not a format to "graduate into" over the course of a 12-week program for the large majority of this population, unlike how VL Body Lab functions as an aspirational next step for other goal categories.
- If a client's actual profile looks more like a Performance-track athlete who happens to be older, have the honest conversation about which base model actually fits them, rather than forcing an athletic client into an Older-Adult Wellness framework or an under-qualified client into VL Body Lab.
- Any coach fielding interest in this format from an Older-Adult Wellness client should treat it as a screening conversation first (training background, clearance specifics, balance and fall history) before any class placement — this is one of the few places in the delivery-format system where the right answer is often "not this format," said plainly.
