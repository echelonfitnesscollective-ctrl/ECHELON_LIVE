# PERFORMANCE IN FAITH & FAVOR MOBILITY

**Base program:** [Performance — 12-Week Base Model](../../05-performance-12-week.md)
**Format:** Restorative strength-and-mobility class · see [Faith & Favor Mobility Blueprint](../../blueprint-03-faith-favor-mobility.md) for the general session shape

---

## How the base model translates here

Faith & Favor Mobility is not where a Performance client builds power, speed, or conditioning — that stimulus has to come from 1-on-1, 12-Week Transformation, or VL Body Lab. What this format delivers for a Performance client is something the base model itself flags as the biggest limiting factor in the entire program: recovery capacity. The base model's own coach notes name recovery — sleep quality, joint health, the body's ability to actually absorb the training stimulus — as more limiting here than in any other base model. Faith & Favor is a direct, purpose-built answer to exactly that need.

**Where this format fits in the picture:**
- As a **recovery complement** to the power/speed/strength days happening elsewhere, positioned the same way as it is for any goal — but arguably more valuable here, given how directly the base model ties recovery quality to the ceiling on power and speed output.
- As **joint-health and mobility maintenance** specifically for the movement patterns the base model stresses hardest — ankle and hip mobility for landing and cutting mechanics, thoracic and shoulder mobility for overhead and pressing work, all of which make the higher-skill Performance movements safer and more effective when they're trained elsewhere.
- **Not** as a starting point or substitute for the base model's actual training days, the way it can be for lower-intensity goals — a Performance client's primary power/speed/strength stimulus cannot be replaced by a restorative class, full stop.

## Personalization decision table

| Intake field | What it changes in Faith & Favor Mobility |
|---|---|
| **Training days available** | For a Performance client specifically, Faith & Favor should be positioned as a genuine *addition* on top of the days needed for power/speed/strength/conditioning work, not a substitute for any of them — if total available days are tight, the power and strength days take priority, and Faith & Favor is the first thing added back once schedule allows. |
| **Current activity level** | A Performance client who's currently deconditioned may benefit from a few weeks of Faith & Favor-style mobility and joint-prep work before jumping into the base model's higher-skill jump/sprint elements — a legitimate short on-ramp here, not a long-term substitute. |
| **Age** | Older Performance clients benefit disproportionately from this format as a standing complement — joint health and mobility directly protect the ability to keep training the higher-impact elements (jumps, sprints, cutting) safely elsewhere in the week. |
| **Injuries, conditions, pregnancy/postpartum** | This is the safest, most appropriate format in the entire lineup for a Performance-goal client managing an injury, or for whom the base model's plyometric/sprint work is paused or off the table entirely (including during pregnancy, per the base model's own guidance) — it keeps them training and moving without the load the base model's power elements require. Any sharp pain is still coached as "ease off, see a clinician." |
| **Medical clearance** | Generally the most permissive format available for a Performance client whose clearance restricts jump, sprint, or heavy-load work specifically — confirm the specific restriction doesn't conflict with any movement in that week's flow, but this is usually where a partially-cleared client can keep training productively while the rest of the plan waits on full clearance. |
| **Sleep and stress** | One of the strongest matches in the whole system, and arguably the single most relevant field for a Performance client in this format — per the base model's own coach notes, sleep quality is the top recovery signal to watch, and this format's breathwork-paired structure directly supports it. |
| **Exercise preferences** | Minor lever; the flow rotates weekly by design, so a disliked single movement is easy to work around. |
| **Equipment access** | Not applicable — the room provides any props needed. |
| **Biggest consistency barrier** | If a Performance client's barrier is burnout or feeling perpetually beat-up from high-intensity training, this format is a legitimate, direct answer — recommending a standing Faith & Favor slot is a concrete, actionable response to that specific barrier, not a generic suggestion to "rest more." |

## Coach notes

- Position this format honestly with a Performance client: it's a genuine, high-value complement to the training that actually builds power and speed, but it is never a substitute for that training — pair it with 1-on-1, 12-Week Transformation, or VL Body Lab rather than presenting it as sufficient on its own for a performance goal.
- Given how directly the base model ties recovery to output quality, a Performance client showing signs of inadequate recovery (per the free-tier and premium recovery/fatigue education content) is a strong candidate to add a Faith & Favor session before their training volume gets cut elsewhere — sometimes the fix for a stalling power block is more recovery support, not less training.
