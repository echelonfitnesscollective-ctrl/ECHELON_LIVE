# PERFORMANCE IN VL BODY LAB

**Base program:** [Performance: 12-Week Base Model](../../05-performance-12-week.md)
**Format:** High-energy athletic group class · see [VL Body Lab Blueprint](../../blueprint-04-vl-body-lab.md) for the general session shape

---

## How the base model translates here

VL Body Lab is this base model's natural home. Per its own blueprint, VL Body Lab is explicitly "the group-format sibling of the individualized Performance 12-week base model", same athletic-development philosophy (power, speed, agility, work capacity), same session logic (warm-up → power/speed block trained fresh and first → strength → conditioning), same non-negotiable coaching standard ("quality over quantity" on every power rep, session cut short the moment output degrades). A Performance-goal client walking into VL Body Lab isn't opting into a mismatch or a scaled-down version of something else, they're opting into a live, group delivery of the exact training philosophy their goal is already built around. This is a direct, strong fit, not an earned-intensity stretch.

**What carries over directly:**
- The base model's day structure maps almost one-to-one onto VL Body Lab's session shape and weekly rotation (see table below).
- The "power trained fresh, first, at maximal intent, full recovery" rule is identical in both, VL Body Lab's blueprint states this using nearly the same language as the base model itself.
- The base model's injury-risk profile and screening standard carry over directly, VL Body Lab is explicitly the highest-intensity group card in the lineup and screens new members accordingly.

**What still gets simplified, honestly:**
- True individualized load/velocity monitoring, a coach watching *one* athlete's jump height rep by rep, isn't fully replicable in a room of members, even with a low coach-to-member ratio. The room compensates with strong live-cued regressions and close attention specifically to the power block, per the blueprint's scaling design principle.
- Sport-specific conditioning is generalized to the room's rotation rather than tuned to one athlete's specific event demands, a client with a narrow, specific competition timeline still benefits from pairing VL Body Lab with 1-on-1 or Transformation for that layer of individualization.

**Mapping the base model's split onto the rotation:**

| Performance base model day | VL Body Lab rotation slot |
|---|---|
| Power + Lower Strength | Week 1 (Lower-Body Power + Lower Strength), near-direct match |
| Speed/Agility + Conditioning | Week 3 (Agility/Change-of-Direction + Total-Body Conditioning), near-direct match |
| Power + Upper Strength | Week 2 (Upper-Body Power + Upper Strength), near-direct match |
| Conditioning + Core/Stability | Folded into the Conditioning Finisher block present in every week's session shape |
| Optional sport-specific skill work | Week 4 (Mixed Format / Team Challenge) is the closest group analog |

## Personalization decision table

| Intake field | What it changes in VL Body Lab |
|---|---|
| **Training experience** | Still the biggest gate, even in this natural-fit format, new/beginner members default to the class's built-in regressions on the Power/Speed block (step-ups, standing throws, shuffles) for their first several sessions, per the blueprint's scaling design. Established-training members can take the full-intensity version from day one. |
| **Age** | Older members benefit from the conservative regression as the default rather than an opt-in, same standard the blueprint sets for the room generally, landing and deceleration mechanics matter more here than in any other block in the class lineup. |
| **Current activity level** | Sedentary or very deconditioned members are better served starting in Group Fitness or a few weeks of base-building before VL Body Lab, even with a genuine Performance goal, the room's baseline intensity assumes some existing conditioning capacity. |
| **Injuries, conditions, pregnancy/postpartum** | Per the blueprint, plyometric and sprint work is not appropriate during pregnancy, and any joint or landing-related pain is coached as "stop immediately, see a clinician", this is identical to the base model's own standard and is never relaxed for a Performance-goal member. |
| **Medical clearance** | Anything short of full clearance is a hard stop for the Power/Speed block specifically; the Strength and Conditioning blocks may still be appropriate with modification, confirm with the coach before class. |
| **Equipment access** | Not applicable, the room's equipment is fixed. |
| **Exercise preferences** | Minor lever, a disliked movement (e.g., a specific throw pattern) is swapped for another movement training the same quality, not dropped outright, since the class rotation is built around hitting each power/strength pattern across the 4-week cycle. |
| **Sleep and stress** | This is the format with the least room for "push through it" of any Performance delivery option, matching the base model's own emphasis that recovery is the biggest limiting factor here, poor sleep or high stress is a direct, immediate cue to take the day's regression, not grind the full-intensity version. |
| **Biggest consistency barrier** | If the barrier is "needing intensity/challenge to stay engaged," VL Body Lab is very likely the right long-term home for a Performance-goal member, name that directly rather than defaulting them to a lower-intensity format out of caution once training readiness is established. |

## Coach notes

- Per the blueprint, screen new members a little more carefully at sign-up here than for Group Fitness or Faith & Favor, a brief injury-history and landing-mechanics check-in before a first class matters more for a Performance-goal member than almost anywhere else in the lineup.
- VL Body Lab is a natural upsell conversation into 1-on-1 or 12-Week Transformation (Performance track) for a member who wants the individualized load monitoring and sport-specific conditioning tuning that a group format honestly can't fully deliver, position that as "more," not as "you're not ready for the group class."
- Unlike the Weight Loss version of this guide, a Performance-goal member doesn't need to "earn" their way into VL Body Lab through prior consistency elsewhere, training readiness (experience, current activity level, clearance) is the gate, not goal-fit.
