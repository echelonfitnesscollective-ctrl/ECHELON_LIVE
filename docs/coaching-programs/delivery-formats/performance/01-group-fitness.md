# PERFORMANCE IN GROUP FITNESS

**Base program:** [Performance: 12-Week Base Model](../../05-performance-12-week.md)
**Format:** Recurring class · see [Group Fitness Blueprint](../../blueprint-01-group-fitness.md) for the general session shape

---

## How the base model translates here: an honest look

Be direct about this one: Group Fitness is not where true Performance training happens, and it shouldn't be positioned as if it is. The Performance base model depends on individualized load and velocity monitoring, power work progressed by watching *this specific athlete's* jump height or bar speed drop, rest periods tuned to *this specific athlete's* recovery between reps, sport-specific conditioning built around *this specific athlete's* actual event demands. None of that is deliverable to a room of 12-15 people on a fixed rotation. A coach cannot watch every member's jump quality closely enough, rep by rep, to make the real-time individualized call the base model requires.

What Group Fitness *can* deliver honestly is a simplified, general-audience version of the base model's philosophy: some exposure to explosive movement patterns, a taste of speed/agility work, and general athletic conditioning, with the coaching standard turned up specifically around safety on the highest-risk movements, since this is the one base model where a room-wide scaling mistake has real injury cost.

**What carries over, what has to be simplified:**
- The base model's movement patterns (jumps, throws, sprints, agility drills, compound strength) translate to Group Fitness in simplified, lower-complexity versions.
- The base model's individualized periodization (Phase 1 → 2 → 3 → Peak) does **not** translate, Group Fitness runs its own 4-week theme rotation instead, not a progressive power block.
- The base model's core rule, power work trained fresh, first, at maximal intent, with full recovery, is the one piece that has to be protected even in a simplified group format, because dropping it is what turns a group version of this program into a genuine injury risk.
- True sport-specific conditioning (the biggest personalization lever in the base model) isn't deliverable in a shared room, Group Fitness offers general athletic conditioning instead.

**Mapping the base model's split onto the rotation:**

| Performance base model day | Group Fitness rotation slot |
|---|---|
| Power + Lower Strength | Week 1 (Full-Body Strength Circuit), with jump/throw variations swapped in at a group-appropriate scale |
| Speed/Agility + Conditioning | Week 2 (Metabolic Conditioning), reframed toward agility ladder and short-interval work rather than pure heart-rate-driven cardio |
| Power + Upper Strength | Week 3 (Strength + Core Focus), with an upper-power element (med-ball throws) added to the strength focus |
| N/A | Week 4 (Mixed Format / Community Challenge) is the natural fit for lighter agility/relay-style athletic games |

## Personalization decision table

| Intake field | What it changes in Group Fitness |
|---|---|
| **Primary/secondary goal** | A Performance-goal member in Group Fitness should have an honest conversation at intake: this format delivers general athletic conditioning, not the individualized power/speed development the base model is built for. If true performance development is the priority, VL Body Lab or 1-on-1 is the better primary format. |
| **Age** | Older members doing the room's jump/throw elements default to the lower-impact regression regardless of stated fitness level, landing and deceleration mechanics matter more with age, and a shared room can't individually verify joint readiness. |
| **Training experience** | New/beginner members are shown the floor-level regression on every explosive movement by default (step-up instead of jump, standing throw instead of running throw); advanced members are offered the scale-up without being asked. |
| **Training days available** | If Group Fitness is a Performance-goal member's only structured training, be clear that it won't produce the results a true 12-week power/speed block would, recommend pairing it with sport-specific work done independently, or moving toward VL Body Lab or 1-on-1. |
| **Equipment access** | Not applicable, the room's equipment is fixed. |
| **Current activity level** | Sedentary/lightly-active members start on the most conservative regression for their first 2-3 sessions on every explosive element, regardless of how eager they are to keep up, this is the format where that discipline matters most given the injury profile of the movements involved. |
| **Injuries, conditions, pregnancy/postpartum** | The single most important field in this format. Flag it to the coach teaching that day *before* class, plyometric and sprint work is not appropriate during pregnancy, and any joint or landing-related pain gets coached as "stop immediately, see a clinician," never worked through. |
| **Medical clearance** | "Pending" or "not yet discussed" caps the member to the room's most conservative regression on every jump/sprint/throw element until resolved, this is not an area where Group Fitness waits for the member to self-advocate mid-class. |
| **Exercise preferences** | Minor lever given fixed room programming, but worth knowing, a member uneasy about jumping can be shown the low-impact swap proactively. |
| **Sleep and stress** | A rough sleep or high-stress week is a direct cue to suggest that day's regression, not the full-intensity version, this format has the least tolerance in the whole Group Fitness lineup for pushing an under-recovered body through explosive work. |
| **Biggest consistency barrier** | If it's "time," Group Fitness's fixed schedule may be the solution, but be honest that it won't fully satisfy a genuine performance goal on its own; that conversation belongs here too. |

## Coach notes

- Screen every new Group Fitness member with a stated Performance goal a little more carefully than a general-fitness member, a brief injury-history and landing-mechanics check before their first jump-based element is worth the extra minute, given this is the highest-injury-risk movement category in the whole class lineup.
- Be honest in the sales conversation: Group Fitness is a legitimate way to get a taste of athletic training and stay consistent, but a member who's serious about measurable performance gains (a faster sprint, a higher vertical, sport-specific readiness) needs 1-on-1, 12-Week Transformation, or VL Body Lab to actually get individualized power/speed programming, steer that conversation early rather than letting expectations mismatch quietly.
