# PREGNANCY IN GROUP FITNESS

**Base program:** [Preconception](../../07-preconception-12-week.md) · [Prenatal](../../08-prenatal-12-week.md) · [Postpartum](../../09-postpartum-12-week.md)
**Format:** Recurring class · see [Group Fitness Blueprint](../../blueprint-01-group-fitness.md) for the general session shape

---

## How the base models translate here

Group Fitness runs on a published, repeating room, it cannot individualize the workout the way 1-on-1 or 12-Week Transformation can, that's true for every goal. For Pregnancy specifically, that limitation matters more, because the standard room programming (jump squats, burpees, prone planks, sprint work) is not automatically appropriate for a prenatal or postpartum client the way a Cutting or Muscle client's standard programming usually is.

**The single most important rule for this format:** the coach must know a member's stage, Preconception, Prenatal, or Postpartum, *before* class starts, not discover it mid-circuit. A pregnancy modification applied after the jump-squat block has already started is too late.

**How each stage actually fits the room:**
- **Preconception** trains like any other Group Fitness member, there are no built-in limits at this stage, full participation in the standard class is appropriate.
- **Prenatal** needs live substitutions applied by a briefed coach: no extended time flat on the back, no high fall-risk or abdominal-impact movements, side-lying/seated/standing variations in place of anything on the floor past the first trimester.
- **Postpartum** needs the coach to know the phase (from the Postpartum base model) before offering any modification, a Phase 1 client should not be doing the room's core or conditioning block at all, regardless of how capable she looks.

## Personalization decision table

| Intake field | What it changes in Group Fitness |
|---|---|
| **Pregnancy stage (Preconception/Prenatal/Postpartum)** | The single biggest gate for this format. This must be flagged to whichever coach is teaching that day *before* class, never discovered mid-session. |
| **Postpartum phase, if applicable** | A Phase 1 or Phase 2 postpartum client should not be attending standard Group Fitness at all, redirect to Faith & Favor Mobility or 1-on-1 until she reaches Phase 3 with no pelvic floor symptoms. |
| **Trimester, if Prenatal** | Second and third trimester clients need the floor-work substitutions (side-lying, seated, standing) offered by default, not as an opt-in. |
| **Medical clearance** | Anything short of full clearance to exercise is a hard stop for Group Fitness specifically, since there is no way to guarantee individualized live coaching in a room of 12-15 people. |
| **Training experience** | Same as any other goal, new/beginner clients default to the floor-level scale-down; this stacks with, not replaces, the pregnancy-specific modifications above. |
| **Sleep and stress** | A rough night or high-stress week is a cue to suggest the lighter version of that day's class, same as any goal, but worth double-checking in with a pregnant or postpartum client specifically since fatigue reporting is often minimized. |
| **Biggest consistency barrier** | If community and consistency are the draw, that's a real reason to keep her in Group Fitness with proper modifications rather than pulling her out entirely, isolation is its own risk during this window. |

## Coach notes

- Build a simple pre-class habit: check any enrolled member's Training Profile for a pregnancy flag before the room fills, not during warm-up.
- If a member's stage or phase means most of a given day's class isn't appropriate for her, say so directly and offer the specific substitution, don't let her guess or default to "just take it easy," vague guidance is how injuries happen in a group setting.
- A Prenatal or Postpartum client who needs substitutions on the majority of a class's movements most weeks is a signal that 1-on-1 or Faith & Favor Mobility will serve her better than Group Fitness for this stretch, not a reason to keep pushing the room format.
