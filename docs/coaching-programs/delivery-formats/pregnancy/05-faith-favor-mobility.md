# PREGNANCY IN FAITH & FAVOR MOBILITY

**Base program:** [Preconception](../../07-preconception-12-week.md) · [Prenatal](../../08-prenatal-12-week.md) · [Postpartum](../../09-postpartum-12-week.md)
**Format:** Restorative strength-and-mobility class · see [Faith & Favor Mobility Blueprint](../../blueprint-03-faith-favor-mobility.md) for the general session shape

---

## How the base models translate here

Faith & Favor Mobility is the lowest-intensity, most restorative card in the lineup, and for Prenatal and Postpartum specifically, that isn't a limitation, it's exactly what those two base models call for. Both programs are built around breathwork, pelvic floor coordination, and gentle, controlled movement as the foundation everything else sits on, which is the entire premise of this class format already.

**Where this format fits by stage:**
- **Prenatal** can use Faith & Favor as a genuine complement across all three trimesters, the class's restorative pace, floor-friendly modifications, and breathwork focus line up directly with the base model's own emphasis on the "talk test" and never pushing past comfortable range.
- **Postpartum**, especially **Phase 1**, this is one of the safest and most appropriate delivery formats available. The class's slow pace and full supervision make it a legitimate primary format in early postpartum, not just a complement to something more intense happening elsewhere.
- **Preconception** can use it the same way any other goal does, a recovery and mobility complement to the strength and cardio days, not a primary training stimulus.

## Personalization decision table

| Intake field | What it changes in Faith & Favor Mobility |
|---|---|
| **Pregnancy stage** | Determines how central this format is: complement for Preconception, strong complement (or primary, depending on trimester and how she feels) for Prenatal, and often the right *starting point* for early Postpartum. |
| **Postpartum phase** | Phase 1 clients are excellent candidates to use this format as their primary movement outlet before Phase 2 strength work begins, it directly overlaps with the breath and pelvic floor work the base model prescribes for this phase. |
| **Trimester, if Prenatal** | Later trimesters benefit especially, as balance and comfort make floor-based, coach-walked, all-levels movement more appealing than higher-intensity formats. |
| **Symptoms that day** | Same rule as every pregnancy-related format, any sharp pain, dizziness, or concerning symptom is coached as "ease off, see your provider," restorative does not mean unsupervised. |
| **Medical clearance** | Generally the most permissive format for a client with clearance restrictions, since intensity is inherently low, still confirm the specific restriction doesn't conflict with a particular movement in that week's flow. |
| **Sleep and stress** | One of the strongest matches in the whole system for this population specifically, new parents and pregnant clients managing poor sleep benefit directly from this format's breathwork-paired structure. |
| **Equipment access** | Not applicable, the room provides any props needed (blocks, straps, chairs). |

## Coach notes

- Position Faith & Favor honestly by stage: for Preconception it's a complement; for Prenatal and especially early Postpartum, it can carry real primary-training weight, don't undersell it as "just the easy class" for clients in those two stages.
- For a Postpartum client starting here in Phase 1, use her time in this format to reinforce the exact skills (diaphragmatic breathing, pelvic floor coordination) that gate her progression to Phase 2, this class and her home program should be reinforcing the same fundamentals, not teaching two different things.
- Revisit her training profile every few weeks the same as any goal, the aim for a Postpartum client starting here is to graduate toward strength work as her phase advances, not to remain here by default once she's cleared for more.
