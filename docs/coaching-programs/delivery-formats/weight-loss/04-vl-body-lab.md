# WEIGHT LOSS IN VL BODY LAB

**Base program:** [Weight Loss — 12-Week Base Model](../../02-weight-loss-12-week.md)
**Format:** High-energy athletic group class · see [VL Body Lab Blueprint](../../blueprint-04-vl-body-lab.md) for the general session shape

---

## How the base model translates here

VL Body Lab isn't the base model's native habitat — it's built around power, speed, and athletic conditioning, while the Weight Loss base model is deliberately lower-intensity and habit-first (RPE 5-7, walk-don't-run cardio, conservative progression). A Weight Loss client showing up to VL Body Lab is opting into a higher-intensity delivery of the same underlying goal, not a mismatch — but it changes what the coach needs to watch for.

**What carries over, what doesn't:**
- The base model's emphasis on full-body strength and conditioning fits VL Body Lab's structure well.
- The base model's walk-based cardio and conservative Phase 1 pacing do **not** carry over as written — VL Body Lab's power/speed block is inherently higher-intensity than anything in the Weight Loss base model's early phases.
- This means a Weight Loss client here needs a heavier reliance on the *scaling*, not the base model's literal numbers.

## Personalization decision table

| Intake field | What it changes in VL Body Lab |
|---|---|
| **Training experience** | This is the single biggest gate for this format. New/beginner clients with a Weight Loss goal should default to the class's built-in regressions on the entire Power/Speed block (step-ups instead of box jumps, standing throws instead of running throws) for at least their first several sessions — this block carries the highest injury risk in the whole class lineup if scaled wrong. |
| **Current activity level** | Sedentary or lightly-active clients are generally not ready for VL Body Lab's power block on day one, regardless of stated goal — steer them toward Group Fitness or a few weeks of base-building first, and revisit VL Body Lab once activity level has climbed. |
| **Age** | Older clients pursuing weight loss through VL Body Lab need the conservative regressions as the *default*, not an opt-in — landing mechanics and joint load matter more here than in any other weight-loss delivery format. |
| **Injuries, conditions, pregnancy/postpartum** | Per the VL Body Lab blueprint, plyometric and sprint work is not appropriate during pregnancy, and any joint or landing-related pain is coached as "stop immediately, see a clinician." A Weight Loss goal never overrides this — redirect to Group Fitness, 1-on-1, or Faith & Favor Mobility instead. |
| **Medical clearance** | Anything short of full clearance is a hard stop for the power/speed block specifically — the strength and conditioning blocks may still be appropriate with modification, but confirm with the coach before class. |
| **Equipment access** | Not applicable — the room's equipment is fixed. |
| **Exercise preferences** | Minor lever, same as Group Fitness — worth knowing so a disliked movement can be swapped for its regression rather than white-knuckled through. |
| **Sleep and stress** | This format has the least room for "push through it" of any weight-loss delivery option — poor sleep or high stress is a direct cue to scale down the power block that day, not grind at full intensity. |
| **Biggest consistency barrier** | If the barrier is "boredom" or "needing intensity to stay motivated," VL Body Lab may genuinely be the right long-term fit once conditioning catches up — worth naming that as the goal even if they start scaled down. |

## Coach notes

- Treat VL Body Lab as an *earned* delivery format for a Weight Loss client, not a default one — screen training experience and current activity level more carefully at sign-up here than for Group Fitness or Faith & Favor.
- A client who's been consistent in Group Fitness or 1-on-1 for several weeks and wants more intensity is the ideal candidate to graduate into VL Body Lab, not someone starting cold.
