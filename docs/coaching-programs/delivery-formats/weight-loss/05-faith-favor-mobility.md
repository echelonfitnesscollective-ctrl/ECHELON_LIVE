# WEIGHT LOSS IN FAITH & FAVOR MOBILITY

**Base program:** [Weight Loss: 12-Week Base Model](../../02-weight-loss-12-week.md)
**Format:** Restorative strength-and-mobility class · see [Faith & Favor Mobility Blueprint](../../blueprint-03-faith-favor-mobility.md) for the general session shape

---

## How the base model translates here

Faith & Favor Mobility is the lowest-intensity card in the lineup by design, and it isn't where a Weight Loss client builds their primary fat-loss stimulus, the base model's strength and conditioning work needs to happen somewhere else (Group Fitness, 1-on-1, 12-Week Transformation, or VL Body Lab). What this format *does* deliver for a Weight Loss client is real, often-overlooked support: recovery capacity, joint health, and the on-ramp that makes the harder training days sustainable.

**Where this format fits in the picture:**
- As a **complement** to a primary training day, same positioning as for any other goal, recovery capacity is what lets someone actually complete a 12-week fat-loss phase without breaking down.
- As a **starting point** for a Weight Loss client whose current activity level, age, or injury history means the base model's regular training days aren't appropriate yet, Faith & Favor becomes the first few weeks of the journey, not a permanent substitute.

## Personalization decision table

| Intake field | What it changes in Faith & Favor Mobility |
|---|---|
| **Current activity level** | Sedentary clients can genuinely start their Weight Loss journey here rather than in a higher-intensity format, this class's all-experience-levels design (floor-level entry on every movement) makes it a legitimate first step, not a consolation prize. |
| **Age** | Older clients pursuing weight loss benefit disproportionately from this format as a standing complement to their primary training days, joint health and mobility directly protect their ability to keep training elsewhere. |
| **Injuries, conditions, pregnancy/postpartum** | This is the safest format in the lineup for a client managing an existing injury or in a postpartum window, but "restorative" doesn't mean unsupervised; the coach still walks the room and corrects alignment, just at a calmer pace. Any sharp pain is coached as "ease off, see a clinician." |
| **Medical clearance** | Generally the most permissive format for a client with clearance restrictions, since intensity is inherently low, still confirm the specific restriction doesn't conflict with any particular movement in that week's flow. |
| **Training days available** | If total available days are limited, weigh whether a day here is better spent on a strength/conditioning day instead, Faith & Favor adds the most value as a *bonus* day, not by displacing limited training time from where the fat-loss stimulus actually happens. |
| **Sleep and stress** | This is one of the strongest matches in the whole system, a client flagging poor sleep or high stress benefits directly from this format's breathwork-paired structure, independent of the weight-loss goal itself. |
| **Equipment access** | Not applicable, the room provides any props needed (blocks, straps, chairs). |
| **Exercise preferences** | Minor lever; the flow rotates weekly by design, so a disliked single movement is easy to work around without restructuring anything. |
| **Biggest consistency barrier** | If the barrier is intimidation or a bad past experience with "intense" training, this format is often the right entry point into Echelon generally, low pressure, welcoming to every level, building the habit of showing up before ramping intensity. |

## Coach notes

- Position this format honestly in the Weight Loss conversation: it's a genuine accelerant and a legitimate on-ramp, but not where the primary fat-loss training stimulus comes from. Pair it with Group Fitness, 1-on-1, or 12-Week Transformation rather than positioning it as a stand-alone weight-loss plan.
- For a client starting here due to low activity level or injury history, revisit their training profile every few weeks, the goal is to graduate them toward a primary strength/conditioning format as capacity builds, not to leave them here indefinitely by default.
