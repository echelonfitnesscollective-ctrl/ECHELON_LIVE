# MUSCLE (HYPERTROPHY) IN VL BODY LAB

**Base program:** [Muscle (Hypertrophy) — 12-Week Base Model](../../04-muscle-hypertrophy-12-week.md)
**Format:** High-energy athletic group class · see [VL Body Lab Blueprint](../../blueprint-04-vl-body-lab.md) for the general session shape

---

## How the base model translates here

This is a genuine mismatch, more so than for most other goal tracks, and it's worth being direct about why. VL Body Lab is built around power, speed, and athletic conditioning — jumps, sprints, throws, and circuit-style strength work moving at a pace designed to keep a room's heart rate elevated. The Muscle (Hypertrophy) base model is built around the opposite emphasis: moderate-to-longer rest periods (45-90 seconds) that allow genuine recovery between sets so load and mind-muscle connection stay high, deliberate tempo and slowed eccentrics, and muscle-group-specific volume tracked and progressed over weeks. Unlike Weight Loss (where VL Body Lab is simply a higher-intensity delivery of the same underlying calorie-burn goal), hypertrophy's actual growth mechanisms don't translate well into this format's structure.

**What carries over, what doesn't:**
- VL Body Lab's Strength Block (15-18 min, compound work) is the only piece that meaningfully overlaps with the base model — it can deliver real mechanical tension on major lifts.
- The circuit-style, group-paced format of that Strength Block typically uses shorter rest and less individualized load selection than hypertrophy training benefits from — a member won't get the same quality of stimulus per set as they would training compounds with proper rest in 1-on-1 or Transformation.
- The Power/Speed Block and Conditioning Finisher contribute essentially nothing toward the base model's specific goal — they're valuable for athleticism and conditioning, but they don't build the kind of targeted muscle size this program is designed for, and pushing hard through them adds fatigue that competes with recovery for the hypertrophy work happening elsewhere in the member's week.
- Mind-muscle-connection coaching and intensity techniques (drop sets, rest-pause, tempo) are not practical to deliver in this format's pace and group structure at all.

## Personalization decision table

| Intake field | What it changes in VL Body Lab |
|---|---|
| **Training experience** | Regardless of experience level, a client whose primary stated goal is precision muscle growth should be told plainly that VL Body Lab is not the right primary format for that goal — steer toward 1-on-1 or 12-Week Transformation, and position this class as a conditioning supplement at most. |
| **Current activity level** | Sedentary or lightly-active clients should not start a hypertrophy goal here at all — build base conditioning and technique elsewhere first (Group Fitness or 1-on-1) before adding VL Body Lab as a supplement. |
| **Age** | Older clients pursuing hypertrophy specifically get little from this format's power/speed emphasis relative to its joint and landing demands — if they want the conditioning benefit, conservative regressions on the Power/Speed Block are the default, not an opt-in. |
| **Injuries, conditions, pregnancy/postpartum** | Per the VL Body Lab blueprint, plyometric and sprint work isn't appropriate during pregnancy, and any joint or landing pain is coached as "stop immediately, see a clinician." A hypertrophy goal never overrides this — and given that this format isn't the primary hypertrophy stimulus anyway, there's little reason to push a client with injury concerns into it at all. |
| **Medical clearance** | Anything short of full clearance is a hard stop for the Power/Speed Block; even with clearance, weigh whether the recovery cost of this format's fatigue is worth it against a hypertrophy-focused member's actual volume needs elsewhere in the week. |
| **Equipment access** | Not applicable — the room's equipment is fixed. |
| **Exercise preferences** | Minor lever — worth knowing so a disliked movement in the Strength Block can be swapped, but this doesn't change the format's fundamental fit for the goal. |
| **Sleep and stress** | Since this format adds conditioning fatigue on top of whatever hypertrophy-specific training the member is doing elsewhere, poor sleep or high stress is a stronger reason than usual to skip or scale down a session — recovery capacity is already the binding constraint in a high-volume hypertrophy block (see the recovery-management article in the education vault), and this format spends that capacity on something other than the member's primary goal. |
| **Biggest consistency barrier** | If the barrier is "boredom" with a more methodical hypertrophy plan, VL Body Lab is a legitimate way to keep a member engaged and moving — just frame it honestly as a supplement to, not a replacement for, their actual muscle-building training. |

## Coach notes

- Don't sell VL Body Lab as a way to build muscle — sell it, honestly, as athletic conditioning and a change of pace for a hypertrophy-focused member who wants variety, with the clear caveat that it competes with recovery capacity rather than contributing to the muscle-growth stimulus.
- A member training a real hypertrophy block elsewhere (1-on-1 or Transformation) who also wants VL Body Lab should have their weekly hypertrophy volume account for the added fatigue — this is a case where two formats stacked together can tip into overreaching faster than either alone.
- If a hypertrophy-focused client is drawn to this format specifically because they want more explosive/athletic training, that's worth a direct conversation about whether Performance, not Hypertrophy, is actually their better-fit primary goal track.
