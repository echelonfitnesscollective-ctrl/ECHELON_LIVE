# ECHELON 12-WEEK BASE MODEL — WEIGHT LOSS

**Goal:** Sustainable fat loss and habit-building for clients starting (or restarting) their fitness journey — distinct from Cutting, which assumes an existing training base and prioritizes preserving competition-level muscle at aggressive intensity. Weight Loss prioritizes consistency, joint-friendly movement, and building the work-capacity foundation everything else is built on.
**Audience:** Beginner–early intermediate, general population, higher body weight or long training layoff, cleared through intake (PAR-Q/health history).
**Format:** 3 full-body lifting days/week + 2–3 walk-based cardio days. Lower weekly density than Cutting on purpose — adherence beats intensity for this population.
**Status:** Base model — personalize load, exercise selection, and walking targets per client before assigning.

---

## 1. Weekly Split

| Day | Focus |
|---|---|
| 1 | Full-Body Strength A |
| 2 | Walk 20–30 min (or rest) |
| 3 | Full-Body Strength B |
| 4 | Walk 20–30 min (or rest) |
| 5 | Full-Body Strength C |
| 6 | Walk 30–40 min + mobility |
| 7 | Rest |

---

## 2. 12-Week Periodization

| Phase | Weeks | Rep Range | Rest | Intensity (RPE) | Focus |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 — Onboard | 1–3 | 12–15 | 60–90s | 5–6 | Movement competency, build the habit, low soreness |
| 2 — Build | 4–7 | 10–12 | 60s | 6–7 | Add load gradually, introduce light circuits |
| 3 — Progress | 8–11 | 8–12 | 45–60s | 7–8 | Full working intensity, daily step target increases |
| Deload | 12 | 12–15 | 60–90s | 5 | Reassess, celebrate wins, plan next block |

**Progression rule:** add reps before adding load. Once a client comfortably hits the top of the rep range with clean form for all sets, add 5–10% load and drop back to the bottom of the range. Never chase load at the cost of form in this population — the goal is a client who wants to come back tomorrow.

---

## 3. Day 1 — Full-Body Strength A

| Exercise | Sets x Reps | Rest | Targets |
|---|---|---|---|
| Goblet Squat | 3 x 12–15 | 60s | Quads, Glutes |
| Seated Row (machine or cable) | 3 x 12–15 | 60s | Back |
| Incline Push-Up (or flat bench press) | 3 x 10–15 | 60s | Chest, Triceps |
| Glute Bridge | 3 x 15 | 45s | Glutes, Hamstrings |
| Assisted or Band-Resisted Plank | 3 x 20–30s | 30s | Core |

**Form cues:** Squat to a comfortable depth, chest tall; row shoulder blades together, not shrugged.
**Coaching cues:** "Own the movement before we add weight" — this population's biggest win is consistency, not week-1 load.
**Modification up:** Add a second slow tempo (3-1-1) round through once form is confident.
**Modification down:** Chair-assisted squat (sit to a box, stand), incline push-up against a wall or counter, knee plank.
**Modification (pregnancy):** Goblet squat only to comfortable depth, avoid supine positions for push-up variations after first trimester (use standing cable press instead), skip prone plank — sub standing pallof press.

---

## 4. Day 3 — Full-Body Strength B

| Exercise | Sets x Reps | Rest | Targets |
|---|---|---|---|
| Leg Press or Split Squat | 3 x 12–15 | 60s | Quads, Glutes |
| Lat Pulldown | 3 x 12–15 | 60s | Back, Biceps |
| Seated Shoulder Press (machine or dumbbell) | 3 x 10–12 | 60s | Shoulders |
| Standing Hip Hinge (light RDL or cable pull-through) | 3 x 12 | 60s | Hamstrings, Glutes |
| Standing Bicycle Crunch (controlled) | 3 x 12/side | 30s | Core |

**Modification down:** Seated leg press instead of split squat if balance is a concern; band pull-apart instead of lat pulldown if grip/shoulder access is limited.
**Modification (pregnancy):** Cap hip hinge load light and stop the movement pattern if it feels compressive; standing bicycle crunch replaced with standing marching in place for gentle core engagement.

---

## 5. Day 5 — Full-Body Strength C (Light Circuit Introduction)

2 rounds through as straight sets, then — once a client has 4+ weeks of consistency — a 3rd round performed as a light circuit (30s work / 30s rest) to start building conditioning capacity without overwhelming a new client.

| Exercise | Sets x Reps | Rest | Targets |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bodyweight or Goblet Squat | 3 x 15 | 45–60s | Quads, Glutes |
| Standing Row (band or cable) | 3 x 15 | 45–60s | Back |
| Wall or Incline Push-Up | 3 x 12–15 | 45–60s | Chest, Triceps |
| Step-Up (low step) | 3 x 10/leg | 45–60s | Glutes, Balance |
| Dead Bug | 3 x 10/side | 30s | Core |

**Coaching cues:** For the circuit round, "pace, don't race" — the goal is elevated heart rate with controlled breathing, not a sprint finish.
**Modification (pregnancy):** Skip the circuit-pace round entirely during pregnancy; keep all three rounds at a controlled, conversational pace with the step height and hip hinge depth reduced as needed.

---

## 6. Walk-Based Cardio (Days 2, 4, 6)

Start at whatever a client can sustain — 15–20 minutes is a completely valid Week 1 starting point. Progress the walk duration (not necessarily pace) by 5 minutes every 2 weeks through Phase 2–3. Day 6's walk can include a few hill or incline minutes once base conditioning improves. This is the highest-leverage, lowest-injury-risk lever in this program — protect it before adding harder conditioning work.

---

## 7. Coach Notes

- **Weekly check-in:** weight trend, 1–2 progress photos, average daily steps, sleep, energy, how many of the planned sessions actually happened (adherence over perfection).
- **The first win to coach toward is consistency**, not a number on the scale — frame early wins around "you showed up 3x this week" before framing them around weight change.
- **Personalization starting points:** joint-friendly substitutions for any client with knee/hip/shoulder history, walking-only weeks if a client needs to rebuild the habit before adding lifting back in, and always confirm nutrition guidance (outside this program's scope, refer out if needed) rather than trying to out-train a poor food environment.
- Pain during any exercise is coached as "stop, and see a clinician" — never worked through or diagnosed on the spot. This population in particular should never be pushed past a comfortable RPE in the name of "no pain no gain" — that mindset is how new clients quit.
