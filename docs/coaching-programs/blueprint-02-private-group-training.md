# TRAINING CARD BLUEPRINT: PRIVATE GROUP TRAINING

**Card copy (live on site):** "A customized Echelon experience for 3–15 friends, families, teams, or organizations, built around your group, goals, and energy."
**Format type:** Recurring private group format, closer to a semi-personalized program than open Group Fitness, since the group is a fixed, known roster rather than drop-in attendees.

---

## 1. Session Shape (45–60 min, set by contract at booking)

| Block | Time | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Warm-Up | 5–8 min | Group-specific, adjust for the group's average fitness/age/goal mix |
| Main Block | 30–40 min | Built around the group's shared goal (see intake below) |
| Finisher | 5–10 min | Optional, scaled to group energy and remaining time |
| Cool-Down | 3–5 min | Stretch, quick group recap of what was covered |

## 2. Group Intake (before the first session)

Unlike open Group Fitness, a Private Group has a knowable roster, so a short intake conversation with the group organizer (and ideally each participant) drives real personalization:
- Primary shared goal (general fitness, an event to train for, a family/friend bonding goal, corporate wellness)
- Age range and any known injury/health flags across the group
- Fitness level spread, is this a matched-level group or a wide range that needs more scaling built in
- Equipment/space available if off-site (relevant for the B2B/apartment-complex delivery model)

## 3. Session Design Principles

- **Design to the group's lowest comfortable fitness level, then build scaling up from there:** the opposite instinct of designing for the most advanced person and scaling down, which alienates newer participants first.
- **Partner and team elements are the format's advantage over solo training:** lean into partner-assisted exercises, relay-style conditioning, and team accountability rather than just running an open-gym class with more people in the room.
- Rotate main-block focus across the engagement (strength week, conditioning week, mobility/recovery week) similar to Group Fitness's 4-week rotation, but adjustable to the specific group's stated goal.

## 4. Live Coaching Cues & Modification Delivery

- Because the roster is known, a coach can genuinely remember each participant's modification from session to session rather than re-assessing cold every time, keep a short mental or written note per person (an equipment-notes-style log is a good home for this if the group is recurring long-term).
- State scale-up/scale-down options before every main-block exercise, same as Group Fitness, but personalize the callout by name where the group size allows it ("Sarah, keep this one at bodyweight this week given your knee").
- Any reported pain is coached as "stop, and see a clinician" for that individual, never pushed through to keep pace with the group.

## 5. Coach Notes

- Confirm session cadence and duration match what was actually contracted (this format is priced/booked per engagement, so scope creep on time or intensity should be flagged, not just absorbed).
- For corporate/B2B groups specifically, keep the tone professional and inclusive of a wide fitness-level spread, this is often someone's first structured training experience, not a fitness-enthusiast crowd.
- End each engagement block with a short recap to the group organizer: what was covered, what's next, any individual flags worth knowing about going forward.
