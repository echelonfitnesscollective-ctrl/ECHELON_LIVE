# ECHELON 12-WEEK BASE MODEL: PRECONCEPTION

**Goal:** Build a strong, well-recovered training and metabolic base for a client actively trying to conceive. This is not a deficit or performance phase, it is the one stretch of the pregnancy path with zero built-in training limits, used to bank strength, pelvic floor coordination, and cardiovascular capacity before those limits arrive.
**Audience:** Clients actively trying to conceive, cleared through intake (PAR-Q/health history), no unmanaged pain flags.
**Format:** 2 full-body strength days a week plus 1 cardio/core day, moderate intensity throughout, always leaving reps in reserve.
**Status:** Base model, personalize load and exercise selection per client before assigning.

## 2. 12-Week Structure

This program does not run on a fixed deload calendar the way the other six do, it holds a steady, moderate structure with one built-in recovery week and a checkpoint at the end.

- **Weeks 1-5:** Full-body strength 2x a week plus one cardio/core day, moderate intensity, always leaving reps in reserve.
- **Week 6:** A lighter recovery week.
- **Weeks 7-11:** Hold the same structure, add load only where it feels good, this is maintenance-of-momentum, not a cut or a bulk.
- **Week 12:** A check-in week, revisit goals and readiness with the client and plan the next block, whether that is another preconception cycle or a transition into the Prenatal program.

**Coaching note:** Avoid coaching toward an aggressive calorie deficit or excessive training volume during this phase, both are linked to disrupted cycles and reduced fertility. Keep intensity in the moderate, sustainable range and prioritize sleep and stress management at every check-in.

## 6. What Sets This Program Apart

Preconception is the one program in the whole system without built-in limits, no trimester adjustments, no pelvic floor gating, no provider-clearance dependency. The discipline here is the opposite: resisting the urge to push toward an aggressive deficit or peak-intensity block just because the body can currently handle it, since fertility itself responds better to moderate, sustainable training than to extremes in either direction.
