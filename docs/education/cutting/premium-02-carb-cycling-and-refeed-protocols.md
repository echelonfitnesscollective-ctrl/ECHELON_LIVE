# CARB CYCLING AND REFEED PROTOCOLS: THE ACTUAL NUMBERS

**Tier:** Premium · Member Vault
**Category:** Cutting Education
**Read time:** ~7 minutes

---

The free-tier article covered what refeeds and diet breaks are and why they exist. This is where that becomes an actual protocol, real timing, real numbers, and the decision rules for when to use a single refeed day versus carb cycling across the week versus a full diet break.

## Carb Cycling: The Basic Structure

Carb cycling means deliberately varying carbohydrate (and often total calorie) intake day to day, tied to training demand, higher carbs on harder training days, lower carbs on rest or light days, while protein stays constant and fat flexes to balance total calories.

**A practical weekly structure for a client training 4-5 days/week:**

| Day type | Carb level | Practical guidance |
|---|---|---|
| Heavy lower-body / high-volume training day | High | Carbs at the top of your daily range, calories at or near the top of your deficit range (smaller deficit that day) |
| Moderate training day | Moderate | Carbs and calories at your standard daily deficit target |
| Rest or light-activity day | Low | Carbs at the bottom of your range, calories at the larger end of your deficit that day, protein and fat held steady |

**Why this works:** total weekly calories and protein stay the same as a flat, unchanging daily deficit would produce, carb cycling doesn't create extra fat loss by itself. What it does is put more fuel where training actually needs it (supporting performance and volume on hard days, which protects muscle per the earlier article) and less where it's least missed (rest days), without changing the total weekly deficit.

## Refeed Protocol: Practical Numbers

A single refeed day, done deliberately rather than reactively:

1. **Bring calories to roughly maintenance** (not above) for that one day, for most clients this means adding back somewhere in the range of **300-600 calories** above their deficit day, almost entirely from carbohydrate.
2. **Keep protein constant.** Protein doesn't drop on a refeed, it's the one macro that shouldn't move.
3. **Let carbs do the work**, typically landing somewhere around **2-3g per pound of bodyweight** for that single day, adjusted to fit within the maintenance-calorie target once protein and a modest fat intake are accounted for.
4. **Frequency:** roughly **once every 1-2 weeks** during a standard cut; more frequently (closer to weekly) during a longer, more aggressive block where the cumulative fatigue is building faster.
5. **Placement:** ideally on your highest-volume training day of the week, so the extra fuel is doing double duty, supporting the psychological reset and directly supporting the session that needs it most.

## Diet Break Protocol: Practical Numbers

For a longer planned pause rather than a single day:

1. **Duration:** 1-2 full weeks at maintenance calories (not a single day, not open-ended).
2. **Calories return to your true maintenance number:** recalculated if you've lost meaningful weight since your last estimate, not just your original pre-cut maintenance.
3. **Macros shift back toward a standard balanced split** rather than staying in a cutting-style macro distribution, this is a real, if temporary, return to normal eating, not a diet in disguise.
4. **When to schedule one:** built into the plan proactively for any cut running longer than roughly 8-10 weeks, or for any stretch running the aggressive end of the deficit range (25-30% under maintenance) for more than 4-6 consecutive weeks, don't wait until performance has already collapsed to schedule it.
5. **Training continues as normal** during a diet break, this is a nutrition pause, not a training deload, though the two can be combined if fatigue markers call for both at the same time.

## Choosing Between the Three Tools

| Situation | Tool |
|---|---|
| One hard week, hunger and fatigue building, otherwise on track | Single refeed day |
| Running a longer or more aggressive block (8+ weeks, or 25%+ deficit) | Carb cycling as the default weekly structure, plus scheduled refeeds |
| Multi-week stall despite honest adherence, or performance/sleep markers clearly declining for 2+ weeks | Full 1-2 week diet break |

These aren't mutually exclusive, a client can run carb cycling as their week-to-week structure and still take a full diet break every several weeks as a larger reset. The common thread across all three: they're planned and bounded, sized to the specific signal they're responding to, not open-ended license to eat freely.

## What This Means for You

- Carb cycling redistributes the same weekly calories and carbs toward training days, it's a distribution tool, not an extra fat-loss lever on its own.
- A single refeed adds roughly 300-600 calories at maintenance, mostly from carbs, protein held constant, placed on your hardest training day.
- A diet break is 1-2 full weeks at true (recalculated) maintenance, scheduled proactively on longer or more aggressive cuts rather than reactively once things are already breaking down.
- Match the tool to the signal: a rough week calls for a refeed; a genuine multi-week stall or declining performance calls for a full diet break.

*This article is educational and general in nature, not individualized medical or nutrition advice. Structuring carb cycling, refeeds, or diet breaks around a medical condition, disordered eating history, or pregnancy should be done with your doctor or a registered dietitian, not from this guide alone.*
