# PROTEIN TARGETS AND TIMING FOR FAT LOSS: THE NUMBERS THAT MATTER

**Tier:** Premium · Member Vault
**Category:** Weight Loss Education
**Read time:** ~6 minutes

---

You know from the free-tier content that protein is the macronutrient that protects your muscle during a fat-loss phase. This is where that turns into an actual number you can hit, and the practical, real-food way to get there without living on chicken and rice.

## How Much Protein Do You Actually Need?

The most useful, research-backed target for someone in a calorie deficit who is also resistance training:

**0.7-1.0 grams of protein per pound of body weight, per day** (roughly 1.6-2.2 g/kg).

A few notes on where you land in that range:
- **Closer to 1.0 g/lb** if you're in an aggressive deficit, have more muscle mass to protect, or are newer to strength training and still building the habit.
- **Closer to 0.7 g/lb** if you're carrying significantly more body fat, protein needs scale more closely with lean body mass than total body weight, so a simplified "per pound of total body weight" target can overshoot for someone with a higher body fat percentage. If that's you, using a modestly lower number in this range, or basing the target on an estimated lean mass instead of total weight, is more accurate.

**A quick example:** a 180 lb client in a moderate deficit lands on a target of roughly 126-180 grams of protein per day. Most people find a number in the middle of their personal range, not the absolute floor or ceiling, is the sustainable long-term target.

## Why This Number, Specifically

Below roughly 0.7 g/lb, research consistently shows more muscle loss during a deficit, even with resistance training in place. Above roughly 1.0 g/lb, additional benefit for muscle preservation flattens out for most people, you're not getting meaningfully more protection, just spending more of your calorie budget on protein instead of other foods you might want. This range is the sweet spot: enough to fully protect your muscle, without needlessly restricting the rest of your diet.

## Does Protein Timing Actually Matter?

Total daily protein matters far more than precise timing, don't let timing anxiety distract from hitting your daily number. That said, two timing principles do have real support and are worth building as habits:

1. **Spread protein across 3-5 meals/snacks** rather than one or two large servings. Your body can only use so much protein for muscle repair at once; spreading intake improves how effectively it's used across the day.
2. **Include protein in your post-training meal**, ideally within a few hours of training. This isn't the mythical "anabolic window" of old bodybuilding lore (that window is far more forgiving than it used to be portrayed), it's simply a practical, easy habit that helps you consistently hit your daily total.

## Real Food, Not Just Supplements

Whole foods should be doing most of the work. A protein shake is a convenient tool for filling a gap, not a requirement. Practical, coaching-approved protein sources to build meals around:

- **Lean meats:** chicken breast, 93%+ lean ground beef/turkey, pork tenderloin
- **Fish and seafood:** salmon, tuna, shrimp, white fish
- **Eggs and egg whites:** genuinely one of the most efficient, affordable protein sources available
- **Dairy:** Greek yogurt (often 15-20g protein per serving), cottage cheese, low-fat milk
- **Plant-based:** tofu, tempeh, edamame, lentils, and legumes (combine with whole grains for a complete amino acid profile)
- **Protein powder:** whey, casein, or plant-based blends, genuinely useful for hitting a gap conveniently, not a substitute for real food across the board

## A Practical Daily Structure

Rather than obsessing over exact grams at every meal, aim for a simple structure:

- **3 main meals**, each built around a palm-to-two-palm portion of a protein source from the list above.
- **1-2 snacks** that include a protein element (Greek yogurt, a protein shake, cottage cheese, jerky) to help close the gap to your daily target.

## What This Means for You

- Target roughly 0.7-1.0 g of protein per pound of body weight per day while in a fat-loss phase.
- Total daily protein matters far more than precise timing, hit the number consistently before worrying about exactly when.
- Spread intake across 3-5 meals and include protein around your training session as a practical habit.
- Build your plan around real food first, using protein powder to fill gaps, not as the foundation.

*This article is educational and general in nature, not individualized medical or nutrition advice. Individuals with kidney conditions or other medical concerns should consult a doctor before significantly increasing protein intake.*
